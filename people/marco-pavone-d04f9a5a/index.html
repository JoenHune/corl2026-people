GLST: Global-Local Spatio-Temporal Memory Decomposition Framework for Online Chained-Goal Navigation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Stanford University / NVIDIA

摘要原文

Autonomous navigation in real-world environments often requires long-horizon operation under partial observability and evolving goals. Existing mapless navigation approaches predominantly assume fixed goals throughout an episode, limiting applicability to practical scenarios where goals change online during execution. We study online chained-goal navigation, where goals evolve through existing-goal redirection or new-goal emergence without explicit transition signals. Under this setting, we find navigation may require either continued exploration or strategic revisitation, creating conflicting memory requirements. We propose Global-Local Spatio-Temporal Belief Learning (GLST), a memory-decomposition framework that separates latent beliefs into global-local spatial and long-short temporal representations for long-horizon reasoning. We further introduce goal-conditioned rewards to balance exploration and exploitation under evolving goals. Extensive experiments in simulation across diverse terrains demonstrate improved chained-goal navigation performance over baselines. Real-robot experiments using the Spot quadruped further validate our memory decomposition framework. We open-source our code for benchmarking.

DIRECT: When and Where Should You Allocate Test-Time Compute in Embodied Planners?

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Stanford University / NVIDIA

摘要原文

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed as high-level planners for embodied agents, with an emerging strategy of scaling test-time compute to improve capability. However, we observe that doing so increases latency, token usage, and FLOPs while yielding uneven, often diminishing gains in downstream success, limiting where embodied agents can be deployed. We argue that choosing when and where to spend test-time compute is central to bringing frontier performance to the real world. We introduce DIRECT, a routing framework that uses multimodal scene context to allocate compute per prompt, improving the success--cost Pareto frontier over fixed model selection. Across three dominant scaling axes, namely chain-of-thought depth, model size, and memory history, our experiments on VLABench and RoboMME show that test-time compute is not a uniform lever: different axes yield qualitatively distinct capability gains. We validate these insights on a physical Franka arm in a DROID setup spanning zero-shot manipulation and long-horizon chaining, where our router matches or exceeds a stronger model's success rate at up to 65% lower average latency. Ultimately, our results show that naively scaling test-time compute is wasteful, and that DIRECT can provide frontier-level embodied planning in robotic systems at a fraction of the cost. Project page can be found at direct-corl.github.io.

Constrained Whole-Body Tracking for Humanoid Robots

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Stanford University / NVIDIA

摘要原文

Recent advances in reinforcement learning (RL) have demonstrated impressive whole-body agility for humanoid robots, yet ensuring safety and satisfying constraints -- particularly those specified after training -- remains a challenge. Towards this goal, we present ConstrainedMimic, a control framework that leverages whole-body kinematics and dynamics for real-time constraint enforcement within RL tracking policies. By integrating principles from operational space control and control barrier functions (CBFs), we enable the satisfaction of arbitrary runtime constraints on both the kinematic reference motion and the underlying dynamics. In whole-body motion-tracking and teleoperation experiments on a (simulated) Unitree G1 with a learned policy, we demonstrate collision avoidance (both with the robot body and external obstacles), joint limits, and center of mass stability constraints. By remaining consistent with the current contact mode and tracking objectives, we minimally restrict the capabilities of the policy when constraints are active. Our method is fully differentiable, runs on CPU, GPU, and TPU, and can be deployed at up to 300-500 Hz. All software will be freely available upon publication.

StressDream: Steering Video World Models for Robust Policy Evaluation and Improvement

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：NVIDIA / Stanford University

摘要原文

Video world models (WMs) have shown promise for policy evaluation and improvement by imagining realistic future observations conditioned on ego-robot actions. While WMs can model distributions over futures, policy evaluation and improvement typically rely on nominal imaginations, which can miss high-impact outcomes of robot actions unless prohibitively many samples are drawn. To enable robust policy evaluation and improvement over WM imaginations, we propose StressDream, which steers imaginations toward high-impact yet plausible outcomes specified at inference time by optimizing the initial noise of diffusion-based WMs. However, optimizing high-dimensional noise is challenging: the optimization must reason about nuanced, scene-dependent target events in generated videos while avoiding out-of-distribution (OOD) noise that yields implausible imaginations. We address this with two complementary objectives: a semantic objective with a Vision-Language Model that provides informative gradients by reasoning about the generated video, and a plausibility objective that prevents the optimized noise from drifting OOD. With state-of-the-art video world models for autonomous driving and robotic manipulation, we show that StressDream effectively steers imaginations toward high-impact yet plausible outcomes specified by text at inference time, such as task failures, enabling robust policy evaluation and improvement by identifying actions whose plausible futures include undesirable outcomes. Video results can be found at https://stressdream.github.io/.
