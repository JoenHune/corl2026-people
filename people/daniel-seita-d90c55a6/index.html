UniLatent: Cross-Embodiment Transfer via Latent Observation Alignment

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Robot datasets are costly to collect and are usually tied to a specific embodiment, making it difficult to reuse demonstrations across robots with different geometry, appearance, and kinematics. We introduce UniLatent, a cross-embodiment transfer framework that avoids pixel-level image translation. UniLatent renders paired source-target robot observations in simulation and aligns a source visual encoder to a fixed target encoder, producing a shared latent space for policy learning. A policy trained on encoded source demonstrations can then be deployed on target observations through the target encoder. Across three simulation tasks and two target embodiments, UniLatent reaches up to 93% zero-shot transfer success (without target demonstrations) and improves the strongest pixel-translation baseline by over 30 percentage-point gain in mean target success. In real-world xArm-to-Franka few-shot transfer, UniLatent completes 27/30 target rollouts, consistently outperforming target-only training and pixel-space translation pretraining.

Beyond the End-Effector: Learning Whole-Arm Contact Manipulation from Human Demonstrations

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Southern California

摘要原文

Robot manipulation has traditionally relied on end-effector-centric interactions. In contrast, humans routinely exploit whole-arm contacts to improve efficiency, robustness, and adaptability, especially when the hands are occupied or when precise grasping is unnecessary. Enabling similar capabilities in robots remains challenging: the robot must select where on its arm to make contact, generate effective non-prehensile interactions, and satisfy task-specific pose or safety constraints on the end-effector. We propose Whole-Arm Contact Learning (WACL), a hierarchical framework that carries each whole-arm contact task to a trajectory optimizer through three robot-agnostic representations: semantic keypoints that summarize the scene, a reference contact trajectory that specifies where and how the arm presses on the surface, and safety-aware path constraints, generated from language by vision-language models, that protect the end-effector's task. The robot's kinematics enters only in a downstream surface-contact optimizer, which grounds these representations onto the arm by jointly searching over which link makes contact, where on that link, and the joint configuration that realizes it. This decomposition lets WACL learn the contact trajectory from a handful of bare-hand human demonstrations, while a new end-effector constraint changes only the optimizer's input rather than any trained component. Real-world experiments on a 7-DoF arm across four whole-arm tasks show that WACL sustains contact under end-effector constraints where a strong keypoint-conditioned baseline fails (78% vs. 33% success), generalizes to new constraints without retraining, and learns without teleoperation. Website: https://wacl-robot.github.io.
