HARP-VLA: Human-Robot Aligned Representation Learning for Vision-Language-Action Model

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Tsinghua University / Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute

摘要原文

Learning generalizable vision-language-action (VLA) models from large-scale human videos is promising but challenging due to cross-embodiment discrepancies in both visual observations and executable actions. While latent action models reduce the action execution gap by learning action abstractions, they still rely on visual features. Thus, misaligned human and robot visual representations can lead to inconsistencies in policy inputs and induce domain-dependent latent actions, hindering effective co-training with human videos. To address this, we propose HARP, a human-robot aligned representation learning framework for more effective VLA pretraining from human videos. Specifically, HARP uses limited paired human-robot demonstrations as cross-embodiment bridges and abundant unpaired human and robot videos as a scalable dynamics supervision data source. It trains a robot-adapted visual encoder and a latent action model with manipulation-centric auxiliary cues and a source-relative pair-discriminative alignment loss, which adapts robot representations toward human semantics while preserving pair-level discrimination. The learned aligned vision encoder and latent action model provide a unified vision and action representation for VLA-style policy learning, where human and robot videos provide vision-language-to-latent-action supervision and a lightweight robot action head grounds latent actions into executable commands. Experiments on feature visualization, simulation, and realworld manipulation show improved human-robot alignment and downstream policy performance, achieving 4.481 average length on CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D and a 7.1\% realworld success rate gain over the strongest baseline.

Learning Additively Compositional Latent Actions for Embodied AI

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Tsinghua University

摘要原文

Latent action models (LAMs) infer pseudo-action labels from visual transitions, providing scalable supervision from internet-scale video for embodied AI. Existing LAMs rely primarily on visual reconstruction, which pushes the latent to encode any cue predictive of the next frame, including background appearance and future shortcuts. The resulting latents entangle motion with nuisance content and their norms decouple from motion magnitude, weakening cross-environment transfer and providing poor supervision for downstream policies. We argue that these failures stem from the absence of structural priors on the latent space, and propose to endow the latent space with an explicit algebraic structure. We introduce the Additively Compositional Latent Action Model (AC-LAM), which imposes a structural constraint on the latent action space, requiring latent actions to compose additively over time. Such additivity is naturally satisfied by rigid-body motion, yet violated by many nuisance signals. We show theoretically that this single constraint induces identity, inverse, and cycle-consistency structure in the latent space, and discourages additively-separable nuisance components such as scene-constant and goal-only offsets. Empirically, we develop a diagnostic suite that probes these algebraic properties and shows that AC-LAM learns more physically meaningful, displacement-calibrated latents with markedly less scene-identity leakage than prior LAMs. The same latents translate into consistent gains on downstream policy learning across both simulated and real-robot tabletop manipulation under in- and out-of-distribution conditions.

HEFT: Heavy Load Full-size Humanoid Teleoperation with Privileged Motion Guidance and Windowed Payload Curriculum

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Tsinghua University / RobotEra / Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute

摘要原文

General motion tracking and teleoperation offer a promising path to scalable humanoid skill acquisition, yet most existing frameworks are validated on compact platforms or without real payload interaction, leaving full-size humanoids with real payloads largely unexplored. Scaling to full-size humanoids introduces two compounding challenges: their larger inertia and tighter balance margins make tracking highly sensitive to noise, drift, and retargeting errors from commodity VR trackers, while their payload potential remains largely underutilized. We present HEFT, a heavy-payload full-size humanoid teleoperation framework that addresses both challenges. HEFT learns from deployable noisy VR references with physically plausible reconstructed references through Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG), and uses a Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC) with expert-guided payload caps to acquire robust heavy-payload tracking. We deploy HEFT on L7, a 175cm, 65kg humanoid. The robot tracks motions including turns, forward/backward locomotion, and squats under payloads up to 24kg.
