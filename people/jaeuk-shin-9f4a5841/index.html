本届研究与论文

TopoUrbanBench: Group-Structural Compliance Evaluation for Urban Robot Navigation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Evaluation practice in social robot navigation rests largely on geometric metrics that treat pedestrians as independent obstacles, assuming social compliance is well-approximated by spatial separation from individuals. As robot navigation transitions to complex urban scenarios, the limits of this assumption become apparent: many pedestrians move within social formations rather than as independent individuals, so a trajectory that keeps safe distance from every individual may still bisect a queue or cut through a gathering's interactional core. To make this gap measurable, we present TopoUrbanBench, an evaluation framework for urban robot navigation. First, we define social structure as an intention-driven graph at generation time and measure trajectory intersection with this graph through three group-structural compliance metrics, one per archetype. Second, built on CARLA, we procedurally generate pedestrian formations anchored to urban semantic features under three archetypes---stationary gatherings, goal-anchored queues, and frame-mobile cohesive walking groups. Third, we apply this framework to evaluate vision foundation models and online RL agents, diagnosing where geometric proficiency and structural compliance diverge. Evaluations on a real-world social navigation dataset highlight the persistent challenges of group-structural compliance, exposing the limitations of current models in physical environments. The full generation pipeline is released to enable extension beyond our predefined scenarios.
