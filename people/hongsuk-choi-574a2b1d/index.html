Flow Policy Gradients for Robot Control

Oral · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Amazon FAR / University of California, Berkeley

摘要原文

Likelihood-based policy gradient methods are the dominant approach for training robot control policies from rewards. These methods traditionally rely on differentiable action likelihoods, which constrain policy outputs to simple distributions like Gaussians. In this work, we show how the recently proposed flow matching policy gradient framework—which bypasses likelihood computation entirely—can be made practical for training more expressive robot control policies, both from scratch and by fine-tuning. We introduce an improved objective that enables success in legged locomotion, humanoid motion tracking, and manipulation tasks, as well as robust sim-to-real transfer on two humanoid robots. We then present ablations and analysis on training dynamics. Results show how policies can exploit the flow representation for exploration when training from scratch, as well as improved finetuning robustness over baselines.

Scalable Behavior Cloning with Open Data, Training, and Evaluation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of California, Berkeley

摘要原文

We introduce ABC, a fully open-source stack for manipulation with behavior cloning. At its core is ABC-Data: the largest open-source, bimanual teleoperation dataset to date, featuring 3,500 hours of data spanning over 130K episodes across 195 diverse tasks. Furthermore, we open-source our accessible hardware setup, training infrastructure, and simulation pipeline. We also release 280 hours of sim-teleop data and provide a co-training recipe that produces correlated simulation and real-world evaluation. This allows researchers to effectively evaluate design choices without deploying on physical robots. We explore various training recipes and compare common architectural choices for Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, grounding our findings in real-world evaluations. The resulting policies successfully execute dexterous tasks such as box folding and extracting credit cards from wallets. By providing a reproducible toolkit, we aim to place researchers on an equal footing, establishing the necessary foundation to learn the ABCs of Behavior Cloning together as a community.
