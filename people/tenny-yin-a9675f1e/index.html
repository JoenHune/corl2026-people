PlayWorld: Learning Robot World Models from Autonomous Play Data

Poster · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：Princeton University

摘要原文

Action-conditioned video models ("world models") are emerging as a promising foundation for data-driven robot simulators that learn world dynamics directly from data. Yet, the role of training data remains poorly understood in shaping their accuracy and reliability. While existing approaches typically train on human demonstrations or task-specific policy rollouts, they exhibit persistent hallucinations in modeling contact-rich manipulation due to systematic biases and insufficient coverage of the training distribution. To address this gap, we present PlayWorld, a new data paradigm for training robot world models from autonomous play. Rather than relying on manual teleoperation or narrow policy rollouts, PlayWorld enables robots to autonomously explore their environments, capturing a broad spectrum of object dynamics and behavioral modes. The resulting world models can simulate diverse policy behaviors with high physical fidelity. We show that PlayWorld enables fine-grained failure prediction and policy evaluation, achieving up to 40% improvements over human-collected data baselines. Using the learned world model for reinforcement learning yields policies that achieve 65% higher real-world success rates upon deployment. To our knowledge, PlayWorld is the first work to demonstrate autonomous play as an effective training paradigm for video world models.

AnyRank: Reliable Anytime Policy Ranking via Sequential Testing

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Princeton University

摘要原文

Due to the high cost of hardware experiments, robot policies are predominantly evaluated by comparing their empirical performance on a limited number of hardware trials. However, finite-sample statistical uncertainty can lead to erroneous conclusions, especially when ranking multiple policies using a fixed batch of evaluation data. We propose \algname: a \emph{sequential} method for policy ranking that (i) \emph{adaptively allocates trials} to hard-to-separate policies, (ii) provides (partial) rankings at \emph{any stopping time} while bounding the probability of an incorrect ranking, and (iii) optionally \emph{exploits prior information} to prioritize hardware evaluations more efficiently using a small set of held-out hardware evaluations or rollouts in a world model. We achieve these properties through novel theoretical contributions that combine sequential and multiple-hypothesis testing. Experiments in simulation and real-world policy evaluation benchmarks show that \algname requires up to 50% fewer hardware evaluations compared to existing statistically rigorous approaches, while guarding against the incorrect rankings produced by heuristic methods used by state-of-the-art benchmarks.

Training Controllable World Models via Action Influence Maximization

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Princeton University

摘要原文

Action-conditioned video generative models promise to serve as general-purpose world models for robotics, leveraging internet-scale data to simulate complex physical interactions. While these models excel at visual realism, they often struggle with controllability--reliably simulating diverse future outcomes from the same state under different action sequences. In this work, we identify a structural cause for this failure: world models trained on successful, goal-directed trajectories tend to ignore actions entirely, collapsing into success-biased video players that hallucinate desirable outcomes regardless of the input action. We characterize this lack of "persistence of excitation" as a collapse in empowerment. To remedy this, we propose an Action Influence Maximization (AIM) objective and Batch Online Reinforcement Learning algorithm that encourages controllability by biasing the training distribution toward state-action pairs that maximize mutual information gain. We evaluate our method extensively for training world models on robotic interaction data on simulated manipulation and navigation tasks. Across our experiments, we find that AIM significantly improves the controllability (and hence, fidelity) of world models in comparison to naively training on expert data. Most notably, world models trained with AIM can outperform the controllability of state-of-the-art world models trained with 85x more data for challenging robotic manipulation tasks.
