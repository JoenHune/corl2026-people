AutoDex: An Automated Real-World System for Dexterous Grasping Data Collection

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Seoul National University

摘要原文

Robotic grasping remains a fundamental challenge, particularly when bridging the gap between simulation and real-world deployment. Although optimization-based simulation methods can generate candidate grasps with strong theoretical validity, their performance often degrades significantly in real-world environments. This degradation is especially severe under challenging conditions such as collisions, clutter, and severe occlusions. In such complex scenarios, grasps produced in simulation are frequently unreliable, leading to high failure rates during physical execution. To address this issue, we propose a system that enhances simulation-verified grasps through a rigorous real-world verification pipeline. Our approach employs a multi-camera setup to automatically validate the physical feasibility of grasps with minimal human intervention. By leveraging multi-view perception, the system can accurately assess grasp validity even under diverse environmental constraints where single-view methods typically fail. Furthermore, we also present a coverage-preserving grasp selection algorithm that ensures the dataset spans a wide range of geometric configurations. We show that this real-world verification process produces substantially more reliable grasps than simulation alone, while also increasing grasp diversity to better handle highly constrained environments. We demonstrate that our automatic pipeline is significantly more efficient and effective than human teleoperation, enabling the collection of a larger and more diverse set of valid grasps. We validate our system across 100 objects with diverse geometries, resulting in a large-scale dataset of firmly grasped, real-world verified objects.

Robot-Factored World Models via Robot Rendering

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Seoul National University

摘要原文

Action-conditioned video world models predict future observations from an initial observation and an action signal. In robotics, this action signal becomes future video in two stages: a robot body and controller realize it as motion, and the scene responds through contact, occlusion, and object motion. Conditioning directly on the pre-realization signal asks the video model to learn robot-specific controller semantics, while conditioning on logged future states leaks interaction outcomes. We propose robot-factored world models, which target the middle signal: a deployment-available nominal trajectory produced by the robot's own controller and kinematics before observing scene interaction. Together with camera-aware static RGB/depth context, this rendered nominal geometry forms a shared visual world-model interface for fixed and dynamic viewpoints. The video model sees the action only as visible nominal geometry, and learns how objects, surfaces, and occlusions change around it. On DROID and RoboCasa-GR1, this rendered interface improves over Cartesian/vector action-conditioning baselines, while motion-source and depth ablations isolate the value of nominalization and 3D contact cues. We further probe the same rendered-prompt interface through counterfactual trajectory redirection, held-out embodiment composition, and human demonstration to robot video.
