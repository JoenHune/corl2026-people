Anchoring Attention in Clutter: Mask-Guided Latent Distillation for Vision-Language-Action Towards Dexterous Grasping

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Dexterous grasping in densely packed same-category clutter remains challenging for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, in part because text-prompted VLAs provide little guidance for selecting among visually identical instances. Although external object masks are commonly provided as visual prompts, making the mask visible does not ensure that the policy maintains focused attention on the masked object---a failure mode especially consequential for dexterous-hand grasping, where small object-grounding errors directly affect contact and closure. We introduce FOCUS, a novel method for Foreground-aware, Object-anchored Continuous grasping that Uses Self-distillation to turn the mask from a passive visual prompt into a training-time alignment signal internalized by the action pathway. During training, FOCUS injects a mask-derived additive bias into the action expert's cross-attention and distills the resulting object-anchored representation into a bias-free pathway, encouraging control to be related to the mask-specified object region rather than object-specific appearance or illumination. To support sequential grasping across multiple objects, a lightweight progress query token co-attends with state and action tokens to trigger sub-task termination and provide phase-aware context. In real-robot experiments at two levels of inter-instance distinguishability, FOCUS achieves 67.5% average grasp success, surpassing all four VLA baselines by at least 26.9 percentage points, with the largest margin (+34.6 points) on the most challenging setting. Additional experiments confirm FOCUS's robustness to unseen object categories (58.4%) and unseen lighting conditions (60.1%), where all four baselines fall below 31%.

TDC: Sim-to-Real Transferable Directional Compliance for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Zhejiang Humanoid Robot Innovation Center

摘要原文

Contact-rich manipulation requires robots to regulate both motion and interaction forces, yet achieving adaptive compliance remains a fundamental challenge. Learning from real-world data is costly and risky, while simulation-based approaches struggle with the sim-to-real gap in contact dynamics; existing sim-to-real methods either require real-world adaptation or sacrifice adaptive compliance by relying on isotropic compliant controllers. Our key insight is that force regulation decomposes into a time-varying but simulation-transferable directional component and a dynamics-sensitive but manually tunable magnitude component. We instantiate this directional component as two policy outputs, a task frame and per-axis control gains, predicted by a visuomotor policy adapted from a pre-trained VLA model and trained via imitation learning on automatically generated simulation demonstrations. At deployment, an admittance controller integrates these predictions with human-specified stiffness and target wrench values to realize adaptive compliance. Our approach achieves adaptive compliance using only simulation data and can benefit from large-scale VLA pre-training. Extensive real-world experiments on four contact-rich tasks, microwave opening, peg-in-hole insertion, whiteboard wiping, and door opening, demonstrate strong task success rates and robustness to external disturbances.
