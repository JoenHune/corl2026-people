本届研究与论文

LCLA: Language-Conditioned Latent Alignment for Vision-Language Navigation

Poster · 首位署名

摘要原文

We propose LCLA (Language-Conditioned Latent Alignment), a framework for vision-language navigation that decouples perception from control by aligning visual-language observations to the latent representation of a frozen privileged expert policy. A lightweight adapter maps frozen vision-language embeddings into the expert latent space using supervised latent alignment rather than end-to-end policy optimization, enabling expert navigation behavior to be reused across perception backbones and environmental variations. We evaluate LCLA across in-distribution simulation, zero-shot out-of-distribution environments, robustness perturbations, and direct real-world deployment on a TurtleBot platform, where it improves OOD success rate from 49.3% to 80.5% and reduces collision rates from 45.0% to 15.5% relative to direct behavior cloning. Without any real-world fine-tuning or calibration, LCLA attains 70.7% real-world success, compared to 60.9% for a navigation-pretrained baseline and 40.2% for direct behavior cloning, demonstrating robust and efficient sim2real transfer while remaining lightweight at inference time.
