本届研究与论文

Self-play is not enough: human imitation improves self-play autonomy in the wild

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Self-play reinforcement learning at scale has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training autonomous driving policies, producing agents that exhibit robust collision-avoidance and stable navigation in simulation. However, policies trained purely through self-play lack exposure to the distribution of real human driving behavior, resulting in trajectories that appear unnatural. To overcome this, we present Self-play with Human Imitation Fine-Tuning (SHIFT), a hybrid training framework that fine-tunes a pretrained self-play foundation policy on real-world driving demonstrations without changing the underlying reinforcement learning algorithm. SHIFT fine-tunes in closed-loop log-replay simulation, in which the ego vehicle is rolled out by the learned policy while the non-ego agents are replayed from logged human trajectories. Distance to the human expert trajectory is simply an additional reward term in the same PPO with advantage filtering algorithm used for pretraining. To enable extended fine-tuning without losing the base self-play performance, we use a KL regularization loss against the frozen self-play foundation policy. On the closed-loop nuPlan Val14 benchmark, SHIFT improves over the self-play baseline in human-likeness (ADE: 9.4 m → 8.0 m, FDE: 21.3 m → 18.5 m), safety (collision rate: 6.4% → 3.9%, drivable area violations: 2.5% → 1.7%, red light violations: 1.6% → 1.0%), and comfort (brake tap rate: 29% → 20%, oscillation rate: 9% → 4%), and achieves the best ADE, FDE, collision, and drivable area scores among all baselines, including extended self-play, behavior cloning, BC+RL, and human-regularized PPO. We deploy both policies on self-driving cars and find similar improvements in the real world (2.6× fewer safety takeovers: one every 0.57 → 1.46 miles). To our knowledge, this is the first evaluation of self-play and self-play with human imitation on public roads. Our results suggest that post-training self-play with human imitation, stabilized by KL regularization, is a promising direction for deployable autonomous driving systems. An open-source implementation, along with additional materials, will be available on the project page: https://rohit-ash.github.io/SHIFT/
