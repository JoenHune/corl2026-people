本届研究与论文

HLO-AMP: Hierarchical Loco-Manipulation from Object-Aware Motion Priors

Poster · 首位署名

摘要原文

Hierarchical pipelines for humanoid loco-manipulation typically pair a task-agnostic whole-body controller (WBC) with a downstream task policy. Such WBCs are often trained through manual reward shaping, which yields visibly machine-like motions; more consequentially, they are trained without the manipulated object in the scene. Object dynamics are instead approximated through randomized end-effector force disturbances, a proxy that fails to reproduce the distributed multi-contact wrench a real payload applies across the arms and torso, with the gap becoming critical near the platform's payload limit. In this work, we train the low-level (LL) controller itself as an object-aware motion prior: a single policy trained via motion imitation on retargeted motion-capture (MoCap) references that span the core motion primitives of the downstream task, with the manipulated object physically present in the scene. This single stage inherits natural human-like coordination from the references and absorbs the realistic contact wrench at its source, while exposing the same compact task-space command interface used by command-tracking WBCs. A reference-free high-level (HL) policy is then trained on top, with its action channel anchored to the empirical envelope of the low-level training data so that it generalizes the priors to a long-horizon multi-stage task while structurally preventing queries that would push the low level off-distribution. We instantiate the framework on a Unitree G1 for bimanual box pick-and-place: the same deployed policy generalizes across object masses and dimensions, initial and goal poses, and reactively recovers from intentional drops mid-carry.
