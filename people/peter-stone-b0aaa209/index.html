ELA: End-to-End Latent Action Policy Learning from Video Demonstrations

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Robot imitation learning is limited by the scarcity of action-labeled data, while action-free demonstrations such as videos are abundant. Recent latent-action methods learn action-relevant representations from observation transitions, but they do not optimize latent actions for downstream control, leading to latent representations that are difficult for policies to model and difficult to accurately decode into continuous robot actions. This paper introduces ELA, an end-to-end framework for learning from video demonstrations via latent actions jointly optimized for policy learning and action decoding. ELA jointly trains a latent action model, latent action policy, and action decoder, enabling policies to leverage abundant video data with only a handful of action-labeled robot demonstrations. Experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks show that ELA consistently outperforms prior latent action baselines. Moreover, it integrates seamlessly with existing imitation learning methods through lightweight 4M-parameter action decoders, requiring no changes to policy architectures or training objectives. Additional materials can be found on: https://end2end-latent-action.github.io/

STARS: From Spatiotemporal Dynamics to Social Representations in Human-Robot Interaction

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Texas at Austin

摘要原文

Fielding socially competent robots requires joint reasoning over spatial and social information. While current models excel at either spatial reasoning (e.g., modeling physical dynamics) or social reasoning (e.g., parsing text-based interactions), few natively unify both. Moreover, data scarcity in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) makes training large models from scratch challenging. To bridge this gap, we introduce STARS: SpatioTemporal Autoencoded Representations for Social-interaction, a method for learning event-level relational representations from HRI datasets. Rather than training task-specific models, STARS models fixed-duration interaction scenarios as spatiotemporal graphs. A self-supervised message-passing graph neural network autoencoder then compresses these physical dynamics into a compact latent space for each node and edge. This injects strong relational inductive bias to naturally capture the underlying social situation. We evaluate STARS' representations on the SEAN Together dataset, which provides VR-based navigation data annotated with subjective human perceptions, and the SocialNav-SUB benchmark for social scene understanding. Linear probing demonstrates that STARS achieves highly competitive performance on perception prediction and pedestrian action classification tasks. Specifically, STARS exhibits excellent data efficiency, achieving an $F_1$-score of 0.635 on predicting perceived competence using just 1% of labeled data, whereas baselines require up to ten times as much. Furthermore, qualitative analysis of the learned latent space reveals that STARS naturally encodes high-level social semantics, demonstrating cohesive clustering of distinct human action intents without explicit task supervision.

Scaled Preference Conditioning for Terrain Costmap Generation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Texas at Austin / Sony AI

摘要原文

Successful autonomous robot navigation in off-road domains requires the ability to generate high-quality terrain costmaps that are able to both generalize well over a wide variety of terrains and rapidly adapt relative costs at test time to meet mission-specific needs. Existing approaches for costmap generation allow for either rapid test-time adaptation of relative costs (e.g., semantic segmentation methods) or generalization to new terrain types (e.g., representation learning methods), but not both. In this work, we present \textit{scaled preference conditioned all-terrain costmap generation} (\textsc{spacer}), a novel approach for generating terrain costmaps that leverages synthetic data during training in order to generalize well to new terrains, and allows for rapid test-time adaptation of relative costs by conditioning on a user-specified \textit{scaled preference context}. Using large-scale aerial maps, we show that \textsc{spacer} achieves the lowest \textit{regret} across varied preferences in five of seven environments for global path planning and generates costmaps from the RELLIS dataset with up to 13\% lower mean absolute error when compared to the ground-truth costmap than the next best methods.

ExpertGen: Scalable Sim-to-Real Expert Policy Learning from Imperfect Behavior Priors

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Texas at Austin / Sony AI

摘要原文

Learning generalizable and robust behavior cloning policies requires large volumes of high-quality robotics data. While human demonstrations (e.g., through teleoperation) serve as the standard source for expert behaviors, acquiring such data at scale in the real world is prohibitively expensive. This paper introduces ExpertGen, a framework that automates expert policy learning in simulation without any human demonstrations, while preserving strong sim-to-real transfer performance. ExpertGen first initializes a behavior prior in using a diffusion policy trained on synthetic data (e.g., Code as Policy). Reinforcement learning is then used to steer this prior in massively parallel simulation toward high task success by optimizing the diffusion model’s initial noise while keeping the denosing process frozen. In this way, ExpertGen regularizes exploration to remain within safe behavior manifolds, enabling stable learning from sparse rewards without additional reward engineering. Extensive experiments on challenging manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that ExpertGen reliably acquires high-quality policies with strong robustness and generalization. On long-horizon manipulation tasks, ExpertGen achieves success rates of up to 85%, while on industrial assembly benchmarks it achieves a 90.5% overall success rate, outperforming all baseline methods. To validate sim-to-real transfer, the learned state-based expert policies are further distilled into visuomotor policies via DAgger and successfully deployed on real hardware.
