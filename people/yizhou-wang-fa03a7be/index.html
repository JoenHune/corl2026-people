Semantic Anchoring for Robotic Action Representations

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Peking University

摘要原文

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models inherit rich semantic representations from pretrained Vision-Language Models, yet fine-tuning on limited robot demonstrations degrades this structure and undermines generalization. A fundamental question therefore arises: what constitutes a good action representation? Inspired by the mirror neuron theory's insight that observation and execution share an intention-level encoding, we examine whether a robot's action representations preserve the semantic structure captured by pretrained encoders. Systematic probing confirms that this structure erodes during fine-tuning, and that its quality synchronizes with both task success and out-of-distribution generalization. We further introduce a plug-and-play method that anchors action representations to a semantic manifold while decomposing representations into a shared semantic channel and a private channel, all discarded at inference, leaving the deployed model unchanged. Validated on different VLA backbones across simulation and real-world benchmarks, our method yields up to +18.7% on real-world in-distribution tasks and +21.5% on out-of-distribution generalization.

Learning Human Intention for Robotic Manipulation

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Peking University

摘要原文

Embodied foundation models have significantly propelled the advancement of artificial general intelligence, yet they rely heavily on large-scale data. Recent studies identify human behavior as one of the most scalable sources for fostering robotic physical intelligence. However, the inherent embodiment gap remains a fundamental challenge for effective human-to-robot learning. In this work, we model human intention to enable efficient learning from human demonstrations. Among human behavioral cues, gaze simultaneously encodes high-level task semantics and exhibits a synergistic coupling with low-level action. By modeling intention through gaze, we derive a mid-level representation that facilitates cross-embodiment knowledge transfer. We demonstrate that robots can internalize human intentions to enhance manipulation, even without robotic intention annotations. To capture the synergy between intention and action, we adopt a Chain-of-Thought reasoning paradigm during inference. Our approach enables the execution of long-horizon and fine-grained tasks across diverse embodiments. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms baseline approaches and generalizes to unseen settings.

WAM-TTT: Learning to Adapt World-Action Models from Human Play at Test Time

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Peking University

摘要原文

Scaling generalist manipulation policies is bottlenecked not by model capacity but by teleoperation cost: covering the full distribution of object placements, orientations, and scene contexts that a deployed robot must handle quickly becomes prohibitive. We argue this bottleneck is largely artificial. Human egocentric demonstrations—plentiful, cheap, and naturally diverse—already encode the visual and physical regularities needed to handle out-of-distribution (OOD) configurations; the missing piece is a recipe that injects this knowledge into a robot-specific action head without re-training the embodiment mapping. Building on LDA-1B, a 1B-parameter latent dynamics-action model pretrained jointly on cross-embodiment robot trajectories and human ego video, we propose a post-training recipe that keeps both data sources alive throughout fine-tuning. Specifically, we co-train with five complementary objectives—policy (P), forward dynamics (FD), inverse dynamics (ID), video generation (VG), and joint action-observation denoising (JD)—routed through embodiment-aware heads. Human ego data participates in FD, ID, VG, and JD but is excluded from policy supervision, so it shapes the shared visual-physical representation while leaving the embodiment-specific action head uncontaminated. We further unfreeze the VLM backbone during post-training, recovering cross-embodiment knowledge that a frozen-VLM recipe otherwise abandons. Across rigorous out-of-distribution evaluations and a suite of long-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks on two robot embodiments, our recipe consistently outperforms robot-only baselines and matches policies trained on substantially more teleoperated data—demonstrating that scarce real-robot demonstrations, combined with abundant egocentric human data, are sufficient for strong generalization.

InfiniDanceRobot: A Streaming and Closed-Loop Music-driven Dance Generation for Humanoid Robot

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Responding to rhythm is a natural human behaviour but a challenge of high-dynamic whole-body control for humanoid robots. Existing motion generation models produce expressive but physically invalid trajectories. As a result, even an expert-trained tracker accumulates a feasibility gap on long sequences of generated dance. In this work, we present InfiniDanceRobot, a latent-guided closed-loop system that streams music-conditioned dance directly to a physical humanoid robot. We collect about 50 hours of dance: 30 hours of lab-controlled motion capture data and 20 hours filtered from public datasets. To provide continuous motion generation ability, we propose a planner building on diffusion forcing with a causal receding noise schedule and cross-modal alignment. Then the latent-guided closed-loop optimization framework is designed to correct next-token predictions from tracker feedback during both training and inference. Experiments show that InfiniDanceRobot can generate expert human dance for the robot, producing continuous, physically plausible, and high-dynamic motion across diverse music styles and dance genres.
