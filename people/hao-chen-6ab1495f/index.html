CoRE-VLA: Towards Scalable and Robust Vision-Language-Action Modeling via Conditional Routing of Experts

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Zhejiang University

按传感器可用性和任务上下文路由不同专家，为 VLA 引入条件化稀疏计算。研究辅助传感器缺失时的稳健退化，以及跨任务、长时操作中的专家专门化。

摘要原文

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have advanced generalist robotic manipulation, yet real-world deployment reveals a fundamental challenge: robots are equipped with diverse and heterogeneous sensor configurations, auxiliary sensors can fail unexpectedly during operation, and different robot embodiments often lack certain sensors by design. A unified policy that can exploit auxiliary perceptual inputs when available while remaining reliable under sensor absence — whether incidental or by design — is therefore essential for practical deployment. However, existing VLA policies couple action generation to a fixed sensor set through shared dense computation, making them brittle when sensors are missing and limiting their ability to specialize across diverse tasks and long-horizon behaviors. We propose CoRE-VLA, a scalable and robust VLA framework that formulates action generation as context-conditioned sparse computation. Sensor availability gates modality-specialized experts, enabling graceful degradation under missing sensors without retraining. Task intent further routes action-side representations to task-relevant experts, improving specialization across diverse tasks and long-horizon subgoals. While the framework is designed to accommodate different auxiliary sensors, we focus on depth as a representative and practically important auxiliary modality in our experiments. Experiments on LIBERO, RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop, and real-world dual-arm manipulation show that CoRE-VLA achieves strong results on long-horizon and multi-task benchmarks, and outperforms both a dense-action-generator ablation and a strong pretrained VLA baseline, including in zero-shot generalization to unseen scenarios. Modality analysis shows that CoRE-VLA can exploit auxiliary depth when available while remaining robust when depth is unavailable during deployment.

Perfect Demo Makes Poor Teacher:Learning Robust Alignment from Critical Motion Segments

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Zhejiang University

摘要原文

Expert demonstrations are often assumed to be the gold standard for robot imitation learning, yet for fine-grained manipulation tasks such as insertion, stacking, and alignment, we find a counterintuitive failure mode: fluent expert demonstrations can be poor teachers. Skilled teleoperators compress the most critical alignment and recovery behaviors into a brief temporal window, leaving imitation models dominated by redundant free-space motion and starved of supervision on the high-precision segments that actually determine success. We study this phenomenon through the lens of critical motion segments and propose three complementary remedies: (i) a deliberate demonstration strategy that slows down near alignment and exposes corrective micro-adjustments, (ii) a resampling scheme that upweights key segments around gripper state changes and task-critical transitions, and (iii) a dynamic feature representation that compresses short temporal neighborhoods of visual observations into predictive features, conditioning the action expert on local motion and alignment progress rather than isolated static frames. Together, these results show that robust robotic alignment benefits less from perfect trajectories than from demonstrations and representations that expose the structure of correction, pointing toward a more pedagogical view of robot data collection where demonstrations are optimized not only for human efficiency but for machine learnability.

GeoWorldAD: Geometry World Action Model for Autonomous Driving

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Zhejiang University

摘要原文

Autonomous driving requires safe and efficient planning in complex, open-world environments. Although recent Vision/Video-Action models learn action-relevant representations directly from visual observations, trajectory planning is fundamentally constrained by scene geometry, such as drivable space, obstacles, dynamic agents, and their spatial relationships. In this work, we propose GeoWorldAD, a unified video geometry world-action model that provides planning with explicit geometric priors over both present observations and future scene evolution. Built upon a streaming video geometry foundation model, GeoWorldAD extracts ego-aligned, multi-scale geometry tokens from current observations to provide fine-grained spatial constraints. A Q-Former-style geometry world model then learns latent future tokens to model short-horizon geometric evolution, supervised by future depth prediction and conditioned on ego states. Compared with pixel-level future prediction, geometry prediction offers a compact, action-relevant abstraction directly aligned with planning constraints. Finally, a geometry-oriented action model iteratively aggregates present and future geometry tokens to refine trajectory proposals, enabling safer and more efficient planning. Extensive experiments on NavSim v1 and v2 demonstrate that GeoWorldAD achieves state-of-the-art closed-loop performance with improved driving safety.
