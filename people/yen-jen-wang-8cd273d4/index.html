VLK: Learning Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from Synthetic Interactions in Reconstructed Scenes

Poster · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：Amazon FAR / University of California, Berkeley

摘要原文

Perception-based humanoid loco-manipulation requires connecting egocentric observations and task instructions to whole-body motion. Learning this mapping requires synchronized egocentric images, language commands, and robot-compatible kinematic trajectories, yet no existing data source provides this complete tuple at scale. We address this bottleneck by generating vision-language-kinematics (VLK) supervision synthetically in reconstructed scenes. Our pipeline leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct metric-scale indoor environments, synthesizes navigation and object-interaction trajectories using privileged scene information, and renders paired egocentric observations after the fact. We produce 48,000 paired trajectories with no human intervention and train a VLK policy that predicts short-horizon whole-body kinematic trajectories. A whole-body tracker converts these predictions into actions on the physical humanoid. We evaluate on the physical Unitree G1 performing navigation and single-object transport, demonstrating that synthesized interactions in reconstructed scenes provide effective supervision for sim-to-real perception-based humanoid loco-manipulation.

ResSafe: Learning Safety Filtering with Residual Reinforcement Learning for Humanoids

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of California, Berkeley

摘要原文

Safe control of humanoid robots remains challenging due to their high-dimensional dynamics, contact-rich interactions, and sensitivity to disturbances. Although reinforcement learning has enabled effective locomotion and motion tracking, learned policies can still generate unsafe actions that lead to instability or falls. In this work, we propose residual reinforcement learning as an implicit safety-filtering mechanism for safe humanoid control. Instead of relying on a single nominal policy to simultaneously balance performance, safety, and robustness, we decouple performance and safety. The nominal policy focuses solely on task performance, while a residual policy learns safety corrections. This decoupling leads to a better performance--safety Pareto trade-off and avoids the need for careful tuning of multiple competing reward terms within a single policy training. We show that the residual policy can act as an implicit safety filter.We evaluate our method in challenging humanoid balance tasks. Compared with the nominal reference policy and a learning-based safety filter baseline, residual reinforcement learning improves safety while still achieving competent performance.

Energy-Based Policy Orchestration

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Hierarchical control scales robot behavior by separating task-level planning from low-level control. In dynamic, high-dimensional tasks, however, a fixed planner-to-policy interface can fail when contacts, balance, or disturbances evolve faster than high-level policy updates. Execution therefore requires choosing not only which low-level policy to run, but also which policy-specific goal should be given to it. We formulate this problem as \emph{policy orchestration}: runtime selection of a policy--goal pair that is likely to remain feasible while tracking the planner target. We develop \emph{EPO}, an energy-based policy orchestrator for modular goal-conditioned policy families. For each policy, EPO learns a composite energy over low-level goals, where lower energy corresponds to higher feasibility and more accurate target realization. At runtime, EPO refines each policy's goal and executes the lowest-energy policy--goal pair. Because energies are policy-specific, new policies can be incorporated by learning their own energy models, without retraining the existing policies or a centralized scheduler. In simulation, EPO improves the empirical precision--robustness trade-off by refining policy-specific goals before selectively switching among policies based on energy scores. We further demonstrate sim-to-real deployment of the same frozen policies and energy models on real humanoid loco-manipulation tasks. Videos are available on the anonymous project website: \url{https://keen-naiad-620edc.netlify.app/}.
