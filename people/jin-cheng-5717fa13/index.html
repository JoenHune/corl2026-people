TARC: Time-Adaptive Robotic Control

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：ETH Zurich

摘要原文

Most robotic systems rely on fixed-frequency discrete-time controllers, creating a trade-off between the efficiency of low-frequency control and the responsiveness of high-frequency feedback. As a result, systems typically default to high control rates for robustness, at the cost of wasted inference bandwidth, actuation jitter, and energy consumption. Addressing this, we introduce Time-Adaptive Robotic Control (TARC), a reinforcement learning framework in which the policy jointly predicts a control action and its duration of application. TARC learns temporally extended actions by optimizing task performance under soft or hard constraints on the number of control switches, enabling adaptive modulation of control rates. We evaluate TARC on two robotic hardware platforms: a high-speed RC car and the Unitree Go1 quadruped, and on a vision-language action model in simulation, where each query incurs a costly transformer forward pass. Across all settings, TARC matches the performance of high-frequency discrete-time controllers while operating at less than half their control frequency. Unlike fixed-rate controllers, TARC adapts its control frequency online, allocating high-frequency feedback only when required.

Teaching Robots Like Dogs: Learning Navigation in Cluttered Scenes from Luring, Gesture, and Speech

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

We propose LURE, a framework that lets users teach a quadruped robot new navigation skills in cluttered scenes by physically luring it with a teaching rod, so that the robot then responds to high-level gesture and verbal commands. Achieving this raises two challenges. First, physical teaching is data-burdensome, and social cues vary substantially across users. Therefore, the system should be able to quickly adapt to each new user. Second, the choice of social-cue modality is critical, as verbal commands specify the action while gestures specify the spatial target, where neither alone is sufficient in our setting. LURE addresses both challenges by combining data augmentation via scene reconstruction and multimodal conditioning. For the data burden, we reconstruct each recorded interaction scene in simulation and learn to recover from deliberately injected perturbations, expanding a few minutes of physical interaction into a larger training distribution. For the modality challenge, we condition the policy on both gesture and verbal cues, and show that the two complement each other, jointly outperforming either modality used alone. Overall, LURE achieves a 97.15% success rate in reconstructed-simulation evaluations of six navigation scenarios in cluttered scenes, using less than one hour of real-world demonstrations. We further show that LURE adapts to a novel user from under five minutes of additional real-world interaction, raising sim-evaluated success by 22.23% on average. We additionally deploy LURE on real hardware for a multi-obstacle course combining training scenes unseen together during training.
