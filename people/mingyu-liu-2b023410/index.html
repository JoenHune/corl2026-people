Perfect Demo Makes Poor Teacher:Learning Robust Alignment from Critical Motion Segments

Poster · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：Zhejiang University / Shanghai Innovation Institute

摘要原文

Expert demonstrations are often assumed to be the gold standard for robot imitation learning, yet for fine-grained manipulation tasks such as insertion, stacking, and alignment, we find a counterintuitive failure mode: fluent expert demonstrations can be poor teachers. Skilled teleoperators compress the most critical alignment and recovery behaviors into a brief temporal window, leaving imitation models dominated by redundant free-space motion and starved of supervision on the high-precision segments that actually determine success. We study this phenomenon through the lens of critical motion segments and propose three complementary remedies: (i) a deliberate demonstration strategy that slows down near alignment and exposes corrective micro-adjustments, (ii) a resampling scheme that upweights key segments around gripper state changes and task-critical transitions, and (iii) a dynamic feature representation that compresses short temporal neighborhoods of visual observations into predictive features, conditioning the action expert on local motion and alignment progress rather than isolated static frames. Together, these results show that robust robotic alignment benefits less from perfect trajectories than from demonstrations and representations that expose the structure of correction, pointing toward a more pedagogical view of robot data collection where demonstrations are optimized not only for human efficiency but for machine learnability.

AdvDex: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Human Demonstrations via Joint-Aligned Actions and Adversarial Learning

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Zhejiang University / Shanghai Innovation Institute

摘要原文

Dexterous manipulation remains a fundamental capability for embodied intelligence, yet its scalability is severely bottlenecked by expensive data acquisition and hardware fragmentation. The current ecosystem lacks a unified data paradigm, preventing the aggregation of demonstrations across structurally diverse robotic platforms. Furthermore, shared visuomotor policies often suffer from visual entanglement, where task-critical spatial semantics are confounded with embodiment-specific appearances. To overcome these barriers, we present AdvDex, a unified Vision-Language-Action framework for scalable cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation. First, we introduce OmniShare, a massive multimodal human demonstration dataset that provides high-fidelity priors to bypass teleoperation bottlenecks. Second, we propose the Joint-Aligned Action Space (JAAS), a canonical $SE(3)$ wrist and 15-finger-joint representation that seamlessly harmonizes human hands, dexterous robotic hands, and parallel grippers. Finally, we employ domain-adversarial learning to explicitly decouple embodiment-specific visual features from universal task skills. Extensive physical experiments demonstrate that AdvDex consistently outperforms single-embodiment baselines, enabling robust zero-shot policy transfer and rapid few-shot adaptation across heterogeneous hardware and unseen environments.
