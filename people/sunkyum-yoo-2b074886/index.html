本届研究与论文

CA-GMT: Contact-Aware Low-Rank Adaptation of Motion Tracking for Whole-Body Teleoperation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

General Motion Tracking (GMT) controllers have advanced humanoid whole-body teleoperation, yet inconsistent foot-ground contact remains a recurring deployment artifact, appearing as foot skating, dragging, and mistimed transitions. Standard kinematic metrics do not fully capture this physical gap, and adapting a frozen GMT backbone to contact-sensitive failures creates a retention--adaptation trade-off shaped by the adaptation parameterization. We study this trade-off through controlled comparisons of full fine-tuning, additive residual adaptation, and low-rank updates under matched supervision and budget, and instantiate the resulting insight in Contact-Aware General Motion Tracking (CA-GMT). CA-GMT combines foot contact-grounded rewards with a low-rank update of a frozen GMT backbone, and includes a phase-conditional variant that restricts adaptation to swing frames. On a held-out evaluation corpus, CA-GMT reduces the False Contact Rate (FCR) from 0.401 to 0.235, while lowering tracking error by 16% and jerkiness by 37%. The phase-conditional variant further reduces FCR to 0.199 while remaining within the base policy's pre-training-distribution variance. Retention analysis across PT/FT/ID/OOD splits shows that the evaluated low-rank parameterization preserves out-of-distribution tracking better than the full fine-tuning and residual variants tested in this work. On a Unitree~G1, the phase-conditional variant again achieves the lowest hardware FCR.
