Visuo-Tactile Policy Learning for Submerged Manipulation

Spotlight · 共同署名

摘要原文

Manipulation in submerged settings (e.g., washing dishes, bathing, collecting water samples) is difficult because of the air-water interface. Water makes visual cues unreliable and damages electronics. We learn visuo-tactile policies for this regime by fusing visual observations with tactile and immersion-depth signals from a sensorized waterproof gripper. We train imitation learning transformer policies that use submerged-domain visual augmentations to reduce overfitting to turbidity, reflection- and refraction-related artifacts. Across the evaluated submerged manipulation tasks, our full policy achieves a mean success rate of 71%, compared to 41% for the best no-tactile variant. This improvement is especially pronounced under difficult visual conditions, such as floating foam and strong glare. Overall, our results demonstrate combining visual robustness with multimodal tactile sensing improves submerged manipulation. See https://davatar-corl26.github.io/ for details.

GUILD: Guided Iterative Data Collection for Imitation Learning

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Imitation learning policies in robotics are trained on datasets collected through costly real-world teleoperation. Often, the data-collection process is unguided, relying on the teleoperator's intuition, resulting in data that contributes little to overall policy performance. Recent work has shown that curating such unguided datasets can substantially improve policy performance, but these approaches operate by filtering existing datasets after collection and therefore do not prevent the acquisition of uninformative data. In this work, we propose a novel Guided Data Collection (GDC) method that actively reasons about which data should be collected to maximize downstream policy performance. We introduce an iterative framework that alternates between 1) task proposal and data collection and 2) data evaluation to close the loop. For task proposal, we leverage semantic priors from vision–language models to identify relevant environments, object configurations, and goals for data collection. However, since semantic relevance does not imply policy improvement, we perform data evaluation. By leveraging predictive data attribution we approximate how the collected trajectories influence policy performance on a target deployment setting. This signal is then used as feedback to guide subsequent data collection, closing the data collection loop. We evaluate our approach in simulation and real robot settings, demonstrating that guided data collection substantially improves sample efficiency and deployment performance compared to passive data collection strategies, multiple baselines and experienced roboticists.
