Workspace Models: Lightweight Robotic Memory via Saliency-Driven Supervision

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Massachusetts Institute of Technology

摘要原文

Complex robotic manipulation tasks frequently require a long-term memory of past events and actions. As conditioning on full histories renders policies prone to spurious correlations and degrades performance, many approaches to policy memory involve compressing historical information through expensive VLM queries in-the-loop to process only task-salient information. In this paper, we propose an alternative approach in which computationally intensive VLM queries are made during train-time to learn a lightweight latent memory that can be efficiently queried at deployment time. Our representation, which we call the workspace token, is trained by (1) using a VLM to identify current and historical information necessary for completing a task, then (2) distilling these into the workspace token using a set-reconstruction decoder loss. In both simulation and hardware, we show that the workspace token can be used as a drop-in replacement for observations during policy deployment, enabling solving memory-intensive tasks without the need for VLM reasoning in-the-loop. Interestingly, we found that workspace tokens are not only more lightweight but also lead to better policy performance.

Unsupervised Actuator Networks as a Residual World Model for Humanoid Control

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Massachusetts Institute of Technology

摘要原文

Humanoid behavior learning uses either physical simulations, which generalize to diverse motions but have limited fidelity, or neural world models, which are highly expressive but data-hungry and brittle. Residual world modeling retains the strengths of both: it preserves the first-principles priors of the physics simulator and only learns the sim-to-real gap. A key challenge when training the residual model for humanoids is accurately initializing the floating base state and contact forces in the simulator to match recorded hardware states. Using the Unsupervised Actuator Networks (UAN) formulation, our method learns a residual correction without torque sensing or motion capture by using a novel proprioceptive state estimator to align initial contact forces and base velocities during open-loop playback of hardware trajectories. This alignment allows the model to capture physics discrepancies rather than artifacts from inconsistent simulator initialization. The resulting residual simulator constrains downstream policy learning to actuator-feasible motions, improving sim-to-real transfer robustness, and enabling efficient locomotion and precise end-effector force control on hardware.
