Humanoid Navigation in Cluttered Environments with a Whole-Body Vision-Language-Action Model

Spotlight · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of California, Berkeley / Peking University

摘要原文

We study the problem of navigating cluttered indoor environments with a humanoid robot. Unlike conventional navigation methods that operate over simplified navigation abstractions, humanoid traversal in cluttered environments requires continuous geometry-aware whole-body adaptation, including coordinated arm placement, torso adjustment, and gait modulation for collision-free movement through complex 3D spaces. We introduce TANGO, the first whole-body vision-language navigation framework for language-conditioned humanoid traversal in cluttered environments. Given a natural-language instruction and egocentric RGB observations, TANGO predicts 29-DoF joint-space actions for whole-body humanoid control. We train TANGO entirely in simulation by synthesizing diverse collision-free traversal behaviors via global path planning, kinematic whole-body motion generation, obstacle-aware motion editing, and reinforcement-learning-based tracking. This pipeline provides dynamically feasible action supervision for learning language-conditioned whole-body policies. In extensive simulation experiments, TANGO outperforms state-of-the-art vision-language-navigation and modular humanoid traversal baselines on long-horizon tasks requiring obstacle negotiation. Lastly, we deploy TANGO zero-shot on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust language-guided traversal in cluttered real-world scenes without training on any real-world data.

SPAN-Nav: Generalized Spatial Awareness for Embodied Navigation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Recent embodied navigation methods leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown strong generalization. However, reliable path planning in complex environments remains challenging due to insufficient spatial awareness. The key challenge lies in learning spatial awareness that generalizes across diverse scenes and supports cross-task navigation reasoning. Motivated by this challenge, we propose SPAN-Nav, which advances generalized 3D spatial awareness for embodied navigation by estimating egocentric 3D occupancy from RGB video streams and using it as an intermediate spatial representation for navigation reasoning. SPAN-Nav represents 3D occupancy in a continuous latent space and projects it into a compact spatial token for VLM integration. This design provides navigation-relevant geometric cues while avoiding the inference overhead of dense volumetric representations. To align occupancy perception with navigation reasoning, SPAN-Nav adopts Two-Stage Spatial-to-Action Alignment (S2A-Align), a training strategy that first grounds navigation decisions on ground-truth occupancy and then adapts action reasoning to self-predicted spatial priors to reduce the training-inference gap. Based on a large-scale dataset with 4.2M occupancy annotations across diverse scenes and navigation tasks, SPAN-Nav achieves state-of-the-art performance on Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), urban navigation and point-goal navigation benchmarks, improving Success Rate (SR) by 4.6% / 5.3% on R2R-CE / RxR-CE, reducing MetaUrban collision cost by over 4×, and reaching 90.9% / 91.0% zero-shot SR on InternVLA-N1 System-1 point-goal navigation benchmark. Real-world experiments further demonstrate robust obstacle avoidance and reliable execution in cluttered environments.
