Trust-Calibrated VLA Policy Post-Training with Evolving Imagination

Poster · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：Sun Yat-sen University / Shenzhen Loop Area Institute

摘要原文

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are often pretrained via imitation learning, making post-training with reinforcement learning (RL) important once deployment moves beyond demonstration coverage. Existing VLA-RL paradigms expose a reliability--efficiency tension. Model-free refinement avoids simulator bias but is costly, static model-based refinement improves efficiency with stale imagination, and online-updated model-based refinement adapts the simulator without auditing generated rollouts. We propose Model-Evolving Reinforcement Learning (MERL), a trust-calibrated framework for VLA policy post-training with simulator-generated imagination. MERL couples policy optimization with an evolving simulator composed of a world model and a reward proxy head, and calibrates imagined rollouts through two-level trust rather than using them uniformly. Across training stages, MERL adjusts the imagined-data ratio and rollout horizon according to simulator reliability; within generated rollouts, it selects and weights reliable imagined chunks before policy optimization. This mechanism keeps refinement close to real-data-driven policy improvement when the simulator is unreliable, and increases the influence of imagination as reliability improves. Experiments on long-horizon perturbed manipulation tasks show that MERL improves final success rate and online learning efficiency over imitation learning, model-free RL, and static or online-updated model-based baselines.

PAWN: Preference-Aware Semantic NFT for Online Reinforcement Learning of Flow-based VLAs

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Online reinforcement learning for flow-based manipulation policies is challenging because multi-step solver-based action generation makes the action likelihoods required by standard policy-ratio objectives difficult to access. Hopefully, NFT-style methods offer a promising likelihood-free route for local policy optimization, and recent embodied extensions have begun to adapt this idea to flow-based robot policies. However, several structural issues remain: trajectory-level outcomes provide noisy and misaligned credit for action chunks and their intermediate solver states; full-space mirrored comparisons ignore the compositional geometry of robot actions; and local updates may still follow noisy behavior-sampled continuations without explicitly identifying better alternatives, which may be guided toward the manifold of suboptimal actions. We present Preference-Aware Semantic NFT (PAWN), a unified NFT-style online RL framework that addresses these issues through structured local preference optimization. PAWN derives implicit chunk-level preference signals by lifting solver-step density comparisons to chunk-path scores, reorganizes mirrored local evidence into SE(3)-aware motion modes, and uses conditional pair guidance to favor locally better continuations. Experiments on flow-based manipulation benchmarks show consistent gains in adaptation efficiency, final success, and OOD robustness over strong online RL baselines.

Building Scalable Real-World Robot Data Generation via Compositional Simulation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Sun Yat-sen University

摘要原文

Recent advancements in foundational models, such as large language models and world models, have greatly enhanced the capabilities of robotics, enabling robots to autonomously perform complex tasks. However, acquiring large-scale, high-quality training data for robotics remains a challenge, as it often requires substantial manual effort and is limited in its coverage of diverse real-world environments. To address this, we propose a novel hybrid approach called Compositional Simulation, which combines classical simulation and neural simulation to generate accurate action-video pairs while maintaining real-world consistency. Our approach utilizes a closed-loop real-sim-real data augmentation pipeline, leveraging a small amount of real-world data to generate diverse, large-scale training datasets that cover a broader spectrum of real-world scenarios. We train a neural simulator to transform classical simulation videos into real-world representations, improving the accuracy of policy models trained in real-world environments. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that our method significantly reduces the sim2real domain gap, resulting in higher success rates in real-world policy model training. Our approach offers a scalable solution for generating robust training data and bridging the gap between simulated and real-world robotics.
