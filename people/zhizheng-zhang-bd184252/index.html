A Systematic Study of Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer in Vision-Language-Action Models

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Galbot

摘要原文

Zero-shot generalization to unseen embodiments is important for generalizable vision-language-action (VLA) models as robot hardware evolves and task-specific data collection remains costly. However, a systematic understanding of this problem remains limited, in part because the literature lacks a unified zero-shot transfer definition and controlled evaluation settings that isolate embodiment changes from differences in tasks, scenes, or protocols. To address this gap, we first distinguishstrict zero-shot transfer, where the target embodiment is absent from all training data, from pretrain-exposed zero-shot transfer, where it appears only during pretraining. We then introduce a controlled benchmark spanning 14 held-out target embodiments across simulation and real-world validation. Within this framework, we systematically study four factors: state-action representations, pretraining embodiment diversity, auxiliary co-training objectives, and target-embodiment exposure. Experimental results show that local end-effector (EEF) state-action representations, the source-diversity comparison, and auxiliary co-training improve cross-embodiment transfer by around 15, 18, and 7 percentage points, respectively. We further find that adding only 5\% target-embodiment data during pretraining improves average target-embodiment progress by 13.4 percentage points, showing that strict and pretrain-exposed zero-shot transfer are distinct and should be reported separately. Together, these findings provide practical guidance for evaluating and improving cross-embodiment VLA transfer.

SPAN-Nav: Generalized Spatial Awareness for Embodied Navigation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Recent embodied navigation methods leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown strong generalization. However, reliable path planning in complex environments remains challenging due to insufficient spatial awareness. The key challenge lies in learning spatial awareness that generalizes across diverse scenes and supports cross-task navigation reasoning. Motivated by this challenge, we propose SPAN-Nav, which advances generalized 3D spatial awareness for embodied navigation by estimating egocentric 3D occupancy from RGB video streams and using it as an intermediate spatial representation for navigation reasoning. SPAN-Nav represents 3D occupancy in a continuous latent space and projects it into a compact spatial token for VLM integration. This design provides navigation-relevant geometric cues while avoiding the inference overhead of dense volumetric representations. To align occupancy perception with navigation reasoning, SPAN-Nav adopts Two-Stage Spatial-to-Action Alignment (S2A-Align), a training strategy that first grounds navigation decisions on ground-truth occupancy and then adapts action reasoning to self-predicted spatial priors to reduce the training-inference gap. Based on a large-scale dataset with 4.2M occupancy annotations across diverse scenes and navigation tasks, SPAN-Nav achieves state-of-the-art performance on Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), urban navigation and point-goal navigation benchmarks, improving Success Rate (SR) by 4.6% / 5.3% on R2R-CE / RxR-CE, reducing MetaUrban collision cost by over 4×, and reaching 90.9% / 91.0% zero-shot SR on InternVLA-N1 System-1 point-goal navigation benchmark. Real-world experiments further demonstrate robust obstacle avoidance and reliable execution in cluttered environments.

FoldNet++: a Large-Scale Synthetic Dataset for Robotic T-Shirt Folding and Unfolding

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Galbot

摘要原文

Due to the highly deformable nature of garments, training a generalizable policy for robotic T-shirt folding and unfolding remains a significant challenge. In this work, we present a large-scale synthetic dataset for robotic T-shirt folding and unfolding, covering 6 robotic embodiments, 1K T-shirts, 1K environmental assets, and 120K episodes with rich annotations, which can be used to train a wide range of manipulation policies. We first follow the FoldNet pipeline to generate a large-scale dataset of physically simulatable T-shirts with diverse appearances and annotated semantic keypoints. Based on these semantic keypoints, we then generate manipulation demonstrations for different robotic embodiments through a unified rule-based framework. We use these demonstrations to train visuomotor policies, and experimental results demonstrate that models trained solely on our synthetic data can achieve over 90\% end-to-end task success rates when directly deployed to unseen real-world environments and previously unseen T-shirts from arbitrary initial configurations.

WAM-TTT: Learning to Adapt World-Action Models from Human Play at Test Time

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Galbot

摘要原文

Scaling generalist manipulation policies is bottlenecked not by model capacity but by teleoperation cost: covering the full distribution of object placements, orientations, and scene contexts that a deployed robot must handle quickly becomes prohibitive. We argue this bottleneck is largely artificial. Human egocentric demonstrations—plentiful, cheap, and naturally diverse—already encode the visual and physical regularities needed to handle out-of-distribution (OOD) configurations; the missing piece is a recipe that injects this knowledge into a robot-specific action head without re-training the embodiment mapping. Building on LDA-1B, a 1B-parameter latent dynamics-action model pretrained jointly on cross-embodiment robot trajectories and human ego video, we propose a post-training recipe that keeps both data sources alive throughout fine-tuning. Specifically, we co-train with five complementary objectives—policy (P), forward dynamics (FD), inverse dynamics (ID), video generation (VG), and joint action-observation denoising (JD)—routed through embodiment-aware heads. Human ego data participates in FD, ID, VG, and JD but is excluded from policy supervision, so it shapes the shared visual-physical representation while leaving the embodiment-specific action head uncontaminated. We further unfreeze the VLM backbone during post-training, recovering cross-embodiment knowledge that a frozen-VLM recipe otherwise abandons. Across rigorous out-of-distribution evaluations and a suite of long-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks on two robot embodiments, our recipe consistently outperforms robot-only baselines and matches policies trained on substantially more teleoperated data—demonstrating that scarce real-robot demonstrations, combined with abundant egocentric human data, are sufficient for strong generalization.

GIF: Agentic Generation of Interactive and Functional Object Compositions for Robot Learning

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Galbot

摘要原文

Embodied foundation models for manipulation require scalable evaluation and data generation over diverse scenarios, with simulation serving as an important pathway. Automated scene generation offers a promising path, yet prior work has largely emphasized coarse-grained scene layouts rather than fine-grained functional object compositions. Motivated by this gap, we present GIF, an agentic Generation framework for Interactive and Functional object compositions. In this framework, we recast this problem as disentangled reconstruction followed by relative pose recovery. CoGen produces instance-disentangled meshes with coarse initial poses leveraging complementary strengths of 2D and 3D generative models, GPRM refines the relative pose under joint geometric and physical guidance, and a VLM verifier selects the candidate that best matches the structured specification. We further construct a benchmark spanning eight representative contact-geometry classes and compare with state-of-the-art generators; GIF improves both asset quality and relation matching, while reducing collision rate to below 1%. Finally, we synthesize data for policy learning, revealing diversity scaling in both simulation and real-world deployment.
