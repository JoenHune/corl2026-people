RoboNaldo: Accurate, Stable and Powerful Humanoid Soccer Shooting via Motion-Guided Curriculum Reinforcement Learning

Oral · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Chinese University of Hong Kong

摘要原文

Elite humanoid soccer shooting requires whole-body stability, high-impulse whole-body interactions, and accuracy to targets. Motion tracking-driven reinforcement learning (RL) provides stability in whole-body movement coordination, but a fixed reference makes it hard to adapt to varied ball positions and strike timings; in contrast, task reward-driven RL struggles to explore and discover valid kicks from scratch. We therefore introduce RoboNaldo, a three-stage motion-guided curriculum RL framework for high-impulse humanoid interaction. A single human-kick reference is used as a scaffold and progressively shifts optimization towards shooting performance. The curriculum first learns stable whole-body kicking prior, then adapts the kick to free-kick settings where the ball is stationary at random positions, and finally extends it to moving-ball shooting through a locomotion-command and kick-trigger interface. A high-level heuristic planner controls this interface during training, while alternative high-level controllers can drive the same low-level policy at inference. In simulation, RoboNaldo demonstrates free-kick shot error 50% lower and shoot velocity 2.96 times than prior work baselines. In real world on a Unitree G1 with onboard perception, RoboNaldo attains 0.73m and 0.86m average target shooting error from 3m away in free-kick and moving-ball cases, accordingly. And the post-contact ball velocity reaches 13.10m/s, which is 59-71% of reported professional open-play shot speed.

HiRE: Hindsight Reward Editing for Policy Finetuning

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Chinese University of Hong Kong

摘要原文

Pre-trained robot policies always require fine-tuning to adapt to new environments. Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers high performance potential because it improves action optimality rather than simply mimicking data. However, such potential depends heavily on reward quality. Sparse rewards lack process feedback, human-designed rewards are costly and biased, and semantic rewards from foundation models are often not control-centric. We propose Hindsight Reward Editing (HiRE), a training-free framework to break this reward bottleneck. HiRE bridges the broad knowledge of foundation representation models with physical control awareness, by contrasting successful and failed trajectories in hindsight. It calibrates foundation representation models with identifying ``trap states'' that are predicted as high-rewarding states yet eventually result in failure, and vice versa. HiRE explicitly penalizes these traps while boosting rewards for critical successful states. This approach can be flexibly compatible with any foundation representations and RL algorithms. Experiments show that HiRE significantly improves fine-tuning performance compared to other reward recipes with minimal computation and high efficiency. Qualitative results are at https://hire-project.github.io.

Humanoid Badminton: Learning Dynamic Racket Skills from Limited Human Motion Data

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Chinese University of Hong Kong / Hong Kong Embodied AI Lab

摘要原文

High-speed racket sports provide a demanding testbed for humanoid robots, where successful interaction requires time-critical decisions, precise striking, and dynamic whole-body coordination. This challenge becomes especially pronounced in badminton, as the shuttlecock follows highly nonlinear trajectories and each return requires tightly coordinated contact selection, racket control, and balance recovery within a short time window. Although human demonstrations offer valuable motion priors for such athletic skills, available badminton data is often limited and imperfect; meanwhile, purely task-driven reinforcement learning can optimize returns but tends to produce unnatural motions that are difficult to deploy on hardware. To address these challenges, we present a three-stage hierarchical framework for athletic humanoid badminton that combines human motion priors with task-driven optimization. The framework first learns a compact latent skill space from retargeted human demonstrations, then optimizes skill composition through reinforcement learning, and finally regularizes the learned behavior with a context-conditioned adversarial latent prior to promote human-like motion. When deployed on a real humanoid robot, our system demonstrates multi-skill badminton play with human players, including rallies and jump smashes. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first real-world demonstration of humanoid jump-smash skills in racket sports.
