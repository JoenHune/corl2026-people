本届研究与论文

DualSpace-VLA: Coupling Cartesian-Space Motion Intent with Joint-Space Execution in Vision-Language-Action Policies

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have made rapid progress in grounding language-conditioned visual observations into robot action chunks. Yet the action interface directly shapes how these chunks are learned and executed on physical robots. Cartesian end-effector space provides an object-centric and geometrically meaningful representation of motion intent, but executing Cartesian actions on joint-controlled robots requires an additional inverse-kinematics layer, which can yield non-unique or less stable joint targets on redundant robots. Joint-space actions are directly compatible with low-level controllers, but can obscure task-level motion structure and require policies to internalize embodiment-specific kinematics. This creates a trade-off for single-space VLAs between clear motion intent and stable execution. To address this trade-off, we introduce DualSpace-VLA, a dual-space VLA framework that uses Cartesian-space motion intent to guide executable joint-space action generation. DualSpace-VLA predicts Cartesian motion-intent tokens from VLM representations under chunk-wise delta end-effector supervision and injects these tokens into a flow-matching joint-action expert through intent-guided cross-attention and gated fusion. Differentiable forward kinematics further maps generated joint trajectories to Cartesian end-effector trajectories, providing a trajectory-level geometric regularizer during training without inverse kinematics at inference time. Extensive experiments in RoboTwin simulation, real-world bimanual manipulation, and few-shot transfer to a low-cost embodiment show that DualSpace-VLA outperforms both end-effector-only and joint-only VLA baselines.
