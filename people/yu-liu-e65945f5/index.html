GeoXel: Geographic Per-pixel 3D Mapping from Aerial Video in the Wild

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

We propose GeoXel, a novel framework for geo-referenced 3D reconstruction from long-duration aerial video. While recent feed-forward reconstruction models have demonstrated impressive efficiency in regressing camera trajectory and 3D geometry, they suffer from two critical limitations: cumulative trajectory drift and the lack of alignment with global geographic coordinates. To address these challenges, we exploit widely accessible geo-referenced geographic data---specifically Digital Orthophoto Maps (DOM), Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and OpenStreetMap (OSM)---as a spatial grounding for precise geo-aligned reconstruction. Specifically, our method decomposes long-sequence videos into overlapping chunks and employs a transformer-based visual geometry model to capture camera trajectory and points map. We then select anchor frames from each video chunk and establish accurate correspondences and geometric transformation constraints between anchor frames and geo-referenced maps to bridge visual reconstruction results and global geographic space. A key contribution of our work is the introduction of a tailored Test-Time Training (TTT) strategy. By fine-tuning a sparse, carefully selected subset of the model's layers, we successfully align the latent tokens with the geographic coordinate system. Our findings reveal that this TTT effectively preserves the reconstructed structural integrity while correcting spatial drift. Furthermore, we conduct intra-chunk and global pose graph optimization (PGO) to ensure local and global spatial consistency. Extensive evaluations on three aerial reconstruction benchmarks demonstrate that our method outperforms existing feed-forward reconstruction baselines by a large magnitude. Our code will be made publicly available upon the publication of this paper.
