本届研究与论文

Progress That Trains: Reference-Anchored Reward Modeling for Robotic Manipulation from a Single Demonstration

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Booking.com

摘要原文

Reinforcement learning for robot manipulation is bottlenecked by reward design: hand-crafted rewards are brittle and prohibitively task-specific, while learned reward models typically demand additional task data and handle failures poorly, leaving them vulnerable to reward hacking. To address this, we introduce the Reference-Anchored Reward Model (RARM), a lightweight visual comparator that leverages a single reference demonstration to generate dense, progress-aware rewards with no per-task reward engineering. RARM couples a DINOv3 backbone with low-rank adapters, a temporal aggregator, and an asymmetric cross-attention comparator, trained once with a contrastive objective on ordinary videos and requiring no robot-specific data. At policy-training time, each rollout clip is matched against reference clips from the demonstration, and the reward is based on confidence-gated progress change from the matched clip, anchoring learning to that single demonstration. Across 10 simulated tasks (LIBERO, MetaWorld) and 4 real-world tasks, including long-horizon settings, RARM achieves the best overall success rates while adding negligible per-step reward generation overhead.
