GeomVLA: Unifying Scene, Motion, and Action in 3D

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Carnegie Mellon University

摘要原文

We present GeomVLA, a 3D motion-aware VLA framework that unifies semantic perception, latent future-motion reasoning, and action generation within a shared robot-centric 3D coordinate frame. Our approach lifts pretrained VLM features into spatially grounded 3D scene tokens while preserving pretrained visual-language priors. We further introduce an Actionable 3D Trajectory Field, a task-conditioned latent motion representation that predicts how task-relevant scene points are expected to evolve directly in 3D space. Rather than serving as an explicit open-loop plan, the predicted trajectory field acts as a latent geometric reasoning process that conditions a 3D flow-based action policy through geometry-aware attention. GeomVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on CALVIN and LIBERO, competitive performance on RoboTwin2.0, and consistently outperforms strong baselines in real-world manipulation settings. Extensive ablations demonstrate that future-motion reasoning alone is insufficient: the primary gains arise from maintaining geometric consistency between scene representation, motion prediction, and robot actions throughout the entire perception-to-action pipeline.

VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Deployable Humanoid-Human Interaction

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Carnegie Mellon University

摘要原文

Humanoids are advancing toward sharing everyday spaces with people, yet reactive humanoid--human interaction (HHI) remains an open question because existing controllers either treat the robot in isolation or engage only static objects and scenes. HHI is challenging because handcrafting an interaction reward is complicated, noisy two-human motion-capture demonstrations cannot be retargeted onto a humanoid without losing their inter-person contacts, and on-board close-range perception breaks down under occlusion and out-of-view human subjects. We present REACT, a learning pipeline that scales HHI to a large corpus of human--human demonstrations. It uses a vision-language model to detect plausible spatio-temporal contacts, and a contact-preserving retargeting stage maps each demonstration onto the humanoid--partner geometry. A privileged-state teacher trained with an interaction contact reward is then distilled into an ego-depth student, with a realistic human partner rendering in the loop so that training-time depth matches the deployed sensor. We also use generative human-pose-estimation auxiliary loss to handle the multimodality of close-range perception. On a real Unitree G1, REACT executes diverse, contact-rich interactions with a human partner, significantly outperforming a baseline that relies on an external pose-estimation module, which becomes inaccurate at close range.

Scaling up Robot Data in Simulation With VLM-Guided Monte Carlo Tree Search over Learned Interaction Skills

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Carnegie Mellon University

摘要原文

Robot manipulation has recently achieved significant progress through large-scale learning from teleoperated demonstrations, but collecting such data remains expensive, labor-intensive, and difficult to scale across diverse environments and long-horizon tasks. We present SkillWeaver, a scalable framework for autonomous manipulation data generation that combines diverse scene generation, reusable RL-based interaction skills, and VLM-guided hierarchical tree search. Given a task and a generated scene, SkillWeaver adaptively decomposes the task into subgoals, executes contact-rich interactions with closed-loop skills, and collects long-horizon demonstrations with failure recovery and re-planning. In contrast to alternative pipelines that rely on predefined sequencing of hand-designed controllers, grasp predictors, and motion planners, our framework adaptively reasons over object geometries and environment configurations, enabling more flexible and robust behavior generation across diverse tasks and scenes. We evaluate \model{SkillWeaver} on LIBERO-PRO and a real Franka robot setup, showing that SkillWeaver effectively scales high-quality manipulation data and that large-scale simulation training enables stronger generalization across novel scenarios.

Dex4D: Task-Agnostic Point Track Policy for Zero-Shot Dexterous Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Carnegie Mellon University

摘要原文

Learning generalist policies capable of accomplishing a plethora of everyday tasks remains an open challenge in dexterous manipulation. In particular, collecting large-scale manipulation data via real-world teleoperation is expensive and difficult to scale. While learning in simulation provides a feasible alternative, designing multiple task-specific environments and rewards for training is similarly challenging. We propose Dex4D, a framework that instead leverages simulation for learning task-agnostic dexterous skills that can be flexibly recomposed to perform diverse real-world manipulation tasks. Specifically, Dex4D learns a domain-agnostic 3D point track conditioned policy capable of manipulating any object to any desired pose. We train this 'Anypose-to-Anypose' policy in simulation across thousands of objects with diverse pose configurations, covering a broad space of robot-object interactions that can be composed at test time. At deployment, this policy can be zero-shot transferred to real-world tasks without finetuning, simply by prompting it with desired object-centric point tracks extracted from generated videos. During execution, Dex4D uses online point tracking for closed-loop perception and control. Extensive experiments in simulation and on real robots show that our method enables zero-shot deployment for diverse dexterous manipulation tasks and yields consistent improvements over prior baselines. Furthermore, we demonstrate strong generalization to novel objects, scene layouts, backgrounds, and trajectories, highlighting the robustness and scalability of the proposed framework.
