KPGrasp: Scalable Keypoint Flow Matching for Dexterous Grasp Generation

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Galbot / Tsinghua University

摘要原文

Generating high-quality dexterous grasps remains challenging for learning-based methods, which often depend on carefully tuned contact losses or costly contact-based test-time refinement. We present KPGrasp, a flow-matching framework that learns dexterous grasp priors from large-scale data rather than relying on contact losses or contact-based test-time refinement. KPGrasp couples an all-Euclidean 3D hand-keypoint parameterization with a simple yet scalable Transformer flow model. The parameterization avoids the drawbacks of the conventional mixed $SE(3)$ pose and joint-angle output space, expresses grasps in the same frame as the object point cloud, and thus enables native spatial reasoning; the Transformer flow model is trained with only the standard flow-matching loss and scales effectively with data, model capacity, and batch size. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on two simulation benchmarks. On the Dexonomy benchmark, it reaches a 76.3\% grasp success rate, improving over the strongest directly comparable baseline by 47.4\% while reducing penetration depth to 2.4 mm. The same model also achieves the best average performance on the DexGrasp Anything benchmark without fine-tuning. For batched inference, KPGrasp requires only 0.032 s per grasp. Finally, real-world experiments on 20 diverse objects demonstrate that the pipeline can be deployed in a real-world setup.

InfiniDanceRobot: A Streaming and Closed-Loop Music-driven Dance Generation for Humanoid Robot

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Responding to rhythm is a natural human behaviour but a challenge of high-dynamic whole-body control for humanoid robots. Existing motion generation models produce expressive but physically invalid trajectories. As a result, even an expert-trained tracker accumulates a feasibility gap on long sequences of generated dance. In this work, we present InfiniDanceRobot, a latent-guided closed-loop system that streams music-conditioned dance directly to a physical humanoid robot. We collect about 50 hours of dance: 30 hours of lab-controlled motion capture data and 20 hours filtered from public datasets. To provide continuous motion generation ability, we propose a planner building on diffusion forcing with a causal receding noise schedule and cross-modal alignment. Then the latent-guided closed-loop optimization framework is designed to correct next-token predictions from tracker feedback during both training and inference. Experiments show that InfiniDanceRobot can generate expert human dance for the robot, producing continuous, physically plausible, and high-dynamic motion across diverse music styles and dance genres.

HumanoidNDM: Closing the Training-Deployment Gap of Motion Tracking via Humanoid Neural Dynamics Model

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Galbot / Tsinghua University

摘要原文

Humanoid robots have recently achieved remarkable success in motion tracking. However, during real-world deployment, robots inevitably encounter dynamics gaps—arising from either extra physical disturbances or inaccurate simulation modeling—resulting in a significant training-deployment gap. Therefore, effectively leveraging real-world data to fine-tune policies represents the crucial ``last mile'' for robust deployment. To bridge this gap, we propose HumanoidNDM, an online fine-tuning framework for humanoid motion tracking driven by a real-world-trained neural dynamics model. First, in the data collection stage, we introduce a low-frequency reference perturbation method with an adaptive intensity mechanism, balancing deployment feasibility and exploration efficiency. Second, for the model training stage, we train a robust history-based, proprioceptive neural dynamics model in Euclidean space by incorporating differentiable forward kinematics, eliminating the reliance on costly motion capture systems. Finally, in the policy fine-tuning stage, we directly fine-tune the policy via backpropagated model gradients, achieving high data efficiency. Through physical validation on the Unitree G1 robot, we demonstrate that HumanoidNDM enables rapid real-world adaptation to various perturbations, representing the first online, MoCap-free fine-tuning framework for humanoid tracking policies.
