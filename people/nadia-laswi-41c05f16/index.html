ScaleEvals: Scaling Generalist Policy Evaluation through Reset-Free Autonomous Play

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Real-world physical evaluation of generalist robot policies remains a major bottleneck: standard protocols require manual scene resets, costly human grading, and extrapolation from small fixed task suites, yielding low throughput and coarse policy diagnostics. We present ScaleEvals, a reset-free evaluation protocol that autonomously probes generalist policies through continuous play without per-rollout human intervention. ScaleEvals uses a VLM-based pipeline in which an AutoPrompter continuously generates grounded tasks and rubrics from an evolving scene, while an AutoGrader assesses task progress from multimodal observations. Each prompt is annotated with skill tags capturing action and language factors, enabling performance analysis across heterogeneous task streams. We normalize comparisons by instruction-derived difficulty and aggregate progress scores via a soft-win Bradley–Terry model with tag-stratified ANOVA, producing global policy rankings and interpretable capability diagnostics. Across five generalist policies and 1,750 real-world trajectories, ScaleEvals achieves a 3x increase in rollouts per hour per station while AutoGrader maintains 90.7% agreement with human graders. Reset-free performance rankings align with manual evaluation baselines with 0.92 (p < 0.05) Pearson correlation, while exposing capability differences that aggregate success rates obscure. These results suggest that real-world robot evaluation can scale as an autonomous, continuously running system rather than a sequence of reset-required benchmark trials.
