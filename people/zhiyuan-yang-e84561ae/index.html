Noise-Invariant Spatial Encoding for 3D Manipulation Policies under Depth Corruption

Spotlight · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：Agency for Science, Technology and Research / Nanyang Technological University

摘要原文

3D manipulation policies derive actions from explicit scene geometry rather than color images alone, enabling them to reason over metric distances, object relations, and robot-scene reachability. This geometric interface is also a vulnerability: commodity RGB-D sensors return missing measurements, boundary artifacts, and plausible but wrong surfaces around transparent, reflective, thin, or textureless objects. We study this failure under realistic depth corruption and observe a sharp depth-noise cliff across modern 3D policies, even when they are trained with matched noisy depth. This residual gap motivates a spatial-encoding approach: corrupted depth becomes entangled with the token coordinates and geometry features consumed by the action expert. We propose Noise-Invariant Spatial Encoding (NISE), a 3D policy architecture that learns trustworthy geometry features even facing imperfect depth. NISE uses Geometric Cross-View Self-Supervision to shape shared policy features with calibrated multi-view depth, and Gaussian Marginalized 3D Attention to carry position uncertainty into deployed attention rather than treating every lifted token as exact. Across simulated benchmarks with sensor-realistic corruption and real-robot tasks involving transparent and reflective materials, NISE improves depth-noise robustness while preserving clean-depth performance, supporting 3D policies designed for commodity RGB-D deployment rather than clean-depth benchmarks alone.

AWLS: Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering for One-Step Generative Robotic Manipulation Policies

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Nanyang Technological University / Agency for Science, Technology and Research

摘要原文

Human-collected real-world robotic manipulation demonstrations often contain mixed-quality trajectories, including hesitant, indirect, or inefficient action segments. While diffusion- and flow-based policies can model such multimodal data, standard behavior cloning (BC) assigns equal likelihood to all demonstrated actions and may therefore reproduce suboptimal modes. Offline RL value estimates offer a natural signal for biasing policies toward higher-value behavioral modes, but directly fine-tuning the generative policy or using critic gradients for policy steering can amplify critic errors and induce prior drift. We propose Advantage-Weighted Latent Steering (AWLS), an offline post-training method for improving frozen one-step generative manipulation policies without updating their decoders. AWLS learns a residual noise shaper, an observation-conditioned residual correction to the policy's latent noise, and trains it with an advantage-weighted action reconstruction objective. The advantages are detached, scale-normalized, and used only as supervised weights on demonstrated actions, avoiding critic-gradient policy updates while preserving fast single-step inference. We further introduce a rollout-free decoder-sensitivity criterion for selecting pretrained checkpoints that remain responsive to latent-noise perturbations. Experiments on simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks with clean and mixed-quality demonstrations show that AWLS improves over the frozen base policy while limiting drift from the pretrained behavior prior.

FR-VLA: Implicit Force Reasoning VLA for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Nanyang Technological University / Agency for Science, Technology and Research

摘要原文

Integrating Vision-Language-Action Model (VLA) with force awareness is not merely a matter of encoding wrench measurements as an additional modality, but of determining the optimal timing and strength when and how physical interaction should influence contact-conditioned robot action generation. Existing force-aware VLAs typically treat force-to-action conditioning as a constant channel, leaving its decision relevance to be inferred implicitly during action prediction. We argue that force-informed action generation requires an intermediate force-relevance mechanism inside the VLA, where visual-language context estimates the task-dependent relevance of physical feedback before force is injected into the action pathway. FR-VLA addresses this gap with a VLM-based implicit force reasoning module that derives a vision-language-grounded force-admission signal from learnable force-query tokens and uses it to modulate an external tokenized force pathway for action generation. Real-world experiments on two robotic platforms and five contact-rich tasks show a 20.8% average success rate improvement over the strongest baseline, with visualizations confirming phase-dependent force admission dynamics.
