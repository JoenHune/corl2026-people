本届研究与论文

Learning Functional Compliance in Whole-body Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Compliant whole-body controllers for humanoids achieve safety by uniformly relaxing stiffness across all directions, but this dissolves the control authority needed for contact-rich tasks, forcing a hard trade-off between safety and performance. We introduce \textit{functional compliance}: a task-dependent compliant control scheme that preserves authority along task-relevant axes while staying compliant elsewhere, varying in both direction and time. We simultaneously learn a control policy using a reward shaped by compliant poses and a force estimator that can be used at deploy time to compose variable compliance profiles. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on three contact-rich whole-body manipulation tasks on the Unitree G1. Our method matches state-of-the-art motion-tracking fidelity while improving task success on key insertion and book reorientation, where directional and time-varying compliance is required, and matching the strongest baseline on cabinet opening, where it is not. This suggests that task-specific variable compliance, not constant uniform relaxation, is the correct whole-body control primitive for humanoid manipulation.
