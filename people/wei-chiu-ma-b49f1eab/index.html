MoRE: Mixture-of-Experts for Multi-Physics Robotic World Models

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Cornell University

摘要原文

A robotic world model should predict how objects evolve under robot actions and physical dynamics. While recent 3D world modeling approaches have made progress toward general robotic prediction, they often struggle to capture the heterogeneous dynamics underlying diverse object interactions. In this paper, we propose MoRE, a novel 3D world model that organizes robotic dynamics into rigid, deformable, and prehensile regimes using a mixture of motion experts built on a shared encoder backbone. This design enables a single model to capture diverse physical behaviors while preserving regime-specific inductive biases. We train a 1B-parameter version of MoRE on a mixture of simulation data generated in Isaac Sim and real-world robotic interaction data. We further integrate MoRE with Model Predictive Control (MPC), enabling a range of manipulation tasks with a unified model. Experiments show that MoRE consistently improves prediction accuracy across rigid, deformable, and prehensile interaction benchmarks, leading to stronger real-world MPC performance.

Proxy Policy Steering

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Cornell University

摘要原文

Robotics foundation models carry broad manipulation priors from large-scale data, but specializing them to a new task remains the deployment bottleneck. This requires injecting new task-specific behavior from limited demonstrations without eroding their broad capabilities. We introduce Proxy Policy Steering (PPS), a novel adaptation method that addresses this challenge by training two lightweight proxy policies. A reference proxy models the frozen base's behavior on target-task observations, and a task proxy, initialized from the reference, captures how this behavior changes under task supervision. Their difference forms a calibrated velocity-space residual that steers the frozen base sampler at every denoising step. We identify the conditions under which this residual isolates the change induced by task supervision, and validate them empirically. On 8 real-world and 4 simulation manipulation tasks, PPS lifts the state-of-the-art $\pi_{0.5}$ base policy by 53% absolute success rate on average, with 0-to-1 gains on tasks the base never solves, while preserving the base's broad capabilities. PPS outperforms LoRA fine-tuning, from-scratch specialists, residual policies, and prior inference-time steering methods.
