An Adaptive Slow-Fast Framework for Real-Time Anomaly Detection in Autonomous Driving

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Anomaly detection is essential for ensuring the safety and reliability of autonomous driving systems operating in open environments, where rare objects, abnormal behaviors, and long-tail events frequently arise. Conventional perception-based approaches often lack the contextual understanding necessary to interpret complex anomalies, while recent Large Language Model (LLM)-based methods incur substantial computational overhead and exhibit limited integration with geometric sensory modalities. To address these challenges, we propose SOFA, an adaptive slow-fast anomaly detection and reasoning framework that selectively activates multimodal reasoning only when anomalous events are detected. SOFA integrates three key components: 1) a fast LiDAR-based anomaly detection module for efficient real-time perception, 2) a slow VLM-based reasoning module for semantic understanding, and 3) a Time-to-Collision (TTC)-driven switching mechanism that dynamically coordinates the two modules to balance efficiency and reasoning fidelity. To enable systematic evaluation of multimodal anomaly detection and reasoning in real-world driving scenarios, we further construct MAQA, a Multimodal Anomaly Question Answering dataset comprising 16K anomalous objects, synchronized LiDAR-image observations, and reasoning-oriented language annotations. Extensive experiments on MAQA demonstrate that SOFA significantly outperforms baselines in detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and reasoning capability.
