W2A: Distilling World Priors into Action Models for Real-Time Robotic Control

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently shown great promise in robotic control, benefiting from the future imagination capability of large-scale pretrained video models. However, most existing WAMs require explicitly imagining future states at test time, incurring substantial computational overhead and latency that make them unsuitable for real-time robotic control. In this paper, we propose W2A, a two-stage distillation framework for real-time robotic manipulation that distills intermediate denoising representations from a video diffusion model into an action model during training, while entirely discarding the video model at test time. We hypothesize that although video latent representations are high-dimensional, a compressed low-dimensional subspace is sufficient to provide effective supervision for action generation. This makes it possible to transfer such information effectively into an action model. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate competitive success rates while significantly improving inference efficiency. On LIBERO, W2A achieves a 95.9% average success rate. More importantly, W2A achieves about 21X faster inference than the representative WAM baseline. Project website: https://anonymous.4open.science/w/W2A.

GSWAM: A Unified Gaussian World Action Model for Robotic Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

World action models (WAMs) provide a promising learning paradigm for robotic manipulation by jointly modeling future observations and robot actions. However, most existing methods operate in 2D image spaces, making it difficult to explicitly capture spatial scene dynamics, maintain cross-view geometric consistency, and effectively align visual observations with robot actions. To address this, we propose GSWAM, a world action model based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which jointly models scene dynamics and robot actions in a unified 3D space. We first compress 3DGS scenes into geometrically consistent latent representations using a Gaussian variational autoencoder (GSVAE). Furthermore, we align scenes and actions within a shared 3D-anchored space, enabling GSWAM to explicitly model the joint distribution between 3D scene dynamics and robot actions. Based on this representation, we design a 3D-anchor-based spatial diffusion transformer to model spatial interactions between scenes and actions. To accommodate their different generative characteristics, we further employ decoupled flow timesteps for the two modalities, allowing the model to jointly learn scene-action generation and action-conditioned future scene prediction. Experiments on both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks demonstrate that GSWAM outperforms representative existing methods in both action generation and future scene prediction. Project website: https://anonymous.4open.science/w/GaussianWAM

ORD-WM: Learning Object-Centric Rigid Dynamics for 3D World Modeling in Robotic Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

World models provide robots with a predictive mechanism for reasoning about how actions change object states in manipulation tasks. However, existing image-based and dense 3D world models do not explicitly impose object-level rigid motion, leaving rigid-object geometry preservation to be learned implicitly and making multi-step rollouts susceptible to shape drift or object-level motion inconsistency. To address this, we propose ORD-WM, a 3D world model that learns object-centric rigid dynamics for robotic manipulation. ORD-WM learns a vector-neuron variational autoencoder supervised by signed distance fields, which encodes each object point cloud as a geometry-aware latent representation composed of 3D vector features, and uses an action-conditioned dynamics model to predict object-wise SE(3) motion increments. Rather than generating unconstrained next latent states, ORD-WM analytically applies predicted object-wise rigid transformations to the current latent states, separating persistent object geometry from pose changes. Experiments show that ORD-WM enables stable, geometry-preserving multi-step prediction and supports efficient real-time replanning for closed-loop model predictive control in simulated and real-world manipulation scenarios. More details and videos are available at: https://anonymous.4open.science/w/ORD-WM.
