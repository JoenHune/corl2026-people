本届研究与论文

Gaze Prompts: Temporally Dense Human Attention for Vision–Language–Action Fine-Tuning

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Tsinghua University

摘要原文

Vision--Language--Action (VLA) fine-tuning supplies dense per-frame visual--action supervision but leaves implicit which part of the scene the demonstrator treated as task-relevant at each timestep---what we call the instruction's \emph{temporal grounding}. We introduce \emph{eye-tracker-supervised gaze prompting} to supply this signal: an HMD eye tracker records the operator's fixation during VR teleoperation, a lightweight predictor (${\sim}50$M parameters) learns to estimate fixation from RGB and language, and the prediction is rendered as a crosshair on the image input of an unchanged $\pi_0$ policy---with no eye tracker or architectural modification at deployment. Across six real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, gaze prompting improves over standard fine-tuning on every task, raising mean success rate from $26.3\%$ to $56.0\%$. Two isolations explain the gains: (i)~with the gaze signal fixed, the input-pixel route carries two signatures of temporal grounding to the policy---instruction-conditional attention and per-frame stage awareness over long horizons---that an auxiliary attention loss on the same gaze does not; and (ii)~under the same route, real human gaze outperforms detection and random cues, with the gap explained by a third signature, anticipation, that engineered cues structurally cannot supply.
