本届研究与论文

Damage-Aware Quadruped Locomotion: Zero-Shot Adaptation to Motor Failures via Pain-Inspired Reinforcement Learning

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Quadruped robots are expected to operate reliably in dynamic and unstructured environments, where unexpected motor failures may occur and severely degrade locomotion performance. Traditional fault-tolerant control methods typically rely on explicit fault detection or handcrafted recovery strategies, which limit their responsiveness and generalization. In this work, we present a damage-aware adaptive control framework that enables quadruped robots to achieve zero-shot locomotion adaptation under sudden motor failures. At its core lies a pain-inspired reinforcement learning paradigm in which the reward explicitly constrains policy behavior, forcing the robot to exploit actuator-health cues from the ontology-sense module, and thereby promoting safer, more protective responses to faults. To improve robustness, we design a failure-probability curriculum that gradually increases the chance of motor failures during training. Evaluations under weakened-motor and locked-joint faults show that the resulting policy achieves lower velocity-tracking errors than SOTA reference across all evaluated fault categories. Comprehensive experiments in both the high-fidelity Isaac Gym simulator and real quadruped hardware demonstrate that our framework achieves robust, damage-aware locomotion, marking a step toward resilient and deployable fault-tolerant legged robots.
