本届研究与论文

TUTOR: Learning Recovery from Policy-Induced Deviations

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Supervised fine-tuning gives vision-language-action (VLA) policies strong manipulation skills in demonstration-like states, but deployment on long-horizon, contact-rich tasks often exposes compounding errors. Small action mistakes can move the robot off the demonstration manifold, where locally plausible action chunks may continue driving the system toward failure. We present TUTOR, a human-in-the-loop post-training method that uses intervention episodes to repair such policy-induced failures. Rather than treating an intervention only as a corrective demonstration, TUTOR also reuses the autonomous rollout before takeover as signed evidence about the policy's behavior. A hindsight keypoint partitions each rollout into recoverable chunks to preserve and failure-inducing chunks to reject, while the human takeover provides verified recovery chunks to imitate. TUTOR trains on these three signals with a negative-aware flow-matching objective. Failed rollout chunks are suppressed by a lagged-reference repulsion term tied to the policy snapshot that generated them, and recovery chunks are fit with standard flow-matching imitation. On a 14-DoF dual-arm tabletop platform with three long-horizon, contact-rich tasks, TUTOR improves autonomous success from 8.3% to 35.0% over supervised fine-tuning, under the same online-episode budget and takeover interface as the intervention baseline. Ablations show that rollout negatives and human recovery data provide complementary gains, while SFT preservation mainly stabilizes updates.
