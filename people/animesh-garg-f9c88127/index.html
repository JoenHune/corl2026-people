MESA: An Evaluation Framework for Compositional, Semantic, and Spatial Generalization in Robotics

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Despite growing interest in foundation models for robotic manipulation, systematic evaluation of their capabilities remains elusive. Existing benchmarks are either narrow and saturated, offering little signal on SOTA methods, broadly scoped, providing diversity at the cost of controlled experimentation, or require training on large, messy open-source datasets. We introduce MESA, an evaluation framework designed for precise, reproducible measurement of language-conditioned policy generalization. MESA provides five evaluation suites that isolate in-distribution performance and four distinct axes of generalization: spatial configuration, object instance, object category, and subtask composition, enabling researchers to diagnose where and why policies fail, not just whether they fail. To populate these suites at scale, we develop MESA-Gen, a pipeline for task and demonstration generation built by improving MimicGen. We instantiate the framework on two platforms and release both: the single-arm MESA suite, and BiMESA, the first large-scale benchmark for bimanual generalization. We evaluate seven baseline methods spanning diffusion policies, VLM-based approaches, and pretrained VLAs, finding that the strongest model achieves only 52% on MESA and 49% on BiMESA, providing meaningful signal while leaving ample room for progress. MESA rankings are broadly consistent with RoboArena scores, suggesting that MESA captures method-level differences relevant beyond simulation.

TAPESTRY: A Single Backbone for Video Generation and Robot Control

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Georgia Institute of Technology

摘要原文

Pretrained video generation models encode rich priors about physical interactions, but leveraging them for robot control typically requires either resource-intensive video generation at test time or action heads trained from scratch that do not fully utilize the pretrained backbone. We introduce Tokenizing Actions as Video Patches in a Shared Transformer Policy (TAPESTRY), a world action model that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a robotic policy without requiring video generation at inference. Our key idea is to treat robot actions as single-patch tokens in the video model's latent space. A lightweight linear projection maps each action timestep to the patch dimensions of the pretrained VAE, and the resulting tokens are processed by the same patchifying layers and transformer backbone used for video. The shared backbone is optimized through three diffusion objectives sampled per minibatch element: Video diffusion uses VAE latents from robot and human videos to establish dynamics priors. Joint video+action diffusion trains on robot demonstrations using a coupled diffusion timestep and a block-causal attention mask. Action-only diffusion denoises actions conditioned on clean image latents. At test time, the model generates a compact action token sequence in a single DDIM step from pure noise, producing smooth action chunks at 84 ms per chunk and avoiding the cost of full video generation. Across simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments, TAPESTRY achieves strong performance with substantially lower inference cost than comparable joint video-action policies. More information can be found at https://tapestry-wam.github.io/.

An Empirical Study of Data-Loading Bottlenecks (and Non-Bottlenecks) in Robotic Imitation Learning

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Modern robotic imitation learning pipelines increasingly rely on large-scale datasets, prompting heavy engineering investments in high-performance data infrastructure under the assumption that dataset loading and online augmentation present primary bottlenecks. We challenge this assumption through a systematic, measurement-driven profile of end-to-end policy training within the LeRobot ecosystem across various spatial resolutions. We evaluate standard implementations against a custom, high-performance Rust data engine utilizing Apache Arrow for zero-copy memory access and a copy-on-write (COW) shared cache. Our profiling reveals three key findings: first, across all reachable configurations on consumer hardware, the data path accounts for a mere 1–4\% of per-step training time; second, substantial data-path optimizations yielding massive isolated micro-benchmark speedups translate to zero measurable wall-clock improvement in full training; and third, severe data-path degradation stems solely from format-specific serialization flaws, such as unindexed sequential video decoding. Finally, we argue that future robotics data infrastructure must be evaluated against overall training step ratios rather than isolated benchmarks, shifting the community's optimization paradigm away from data loading and toward upstream model layers like visual feature caching.
