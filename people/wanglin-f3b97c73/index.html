Decision-Centric Counterfactual Preference Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Post-Training

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are increasingly post-trained with rollout-level feedback, but a failed language-conditioned trajectory often hinges on a local decision that the terminal success label does not identify. World models make post-training scalable through imagined rollouts, yet treating the whole trajectory as the supervision unit still mixes object grounding, target selection, contact alignment, and recovery into one outcome. We introduce Decision-Centric Counterfactual Preference Optimization (DCCP), a world-model post-training method that converts imagined futures into localized action preferences. DCCP mines decision-sensitive states using progress curvature and action-token entropy, intervenes only on the current first action chunk under a shared visual-language prefix, and evaluates short counterfactual branches with a conditional progress scorer. High-margin branch differences become action-token preferences optimized by a local DPO-style loss, while a trajectory-level GRPO objective preserves full-task improvement. Across standard LIBERO, LIBERO-BDDL, and MimicGen-D0, DCCP improves average success from 75.6% with WMPO to 80.1%, with the largest gains on instruction-sensitive branch-selection tasks. On four RM65 real-robot tasks, DCCP improves success from 70.6% to 78.8% over WMPO and reduces semantic commitment errors from 13.1% to 7.5%. These results show that world models can provide not only more rollouts, but sharper credit for the action choices that determine downstream progress.
