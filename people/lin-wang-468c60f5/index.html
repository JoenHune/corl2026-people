RoboAffect: Egocentric Emotion Reasoning for Robot Companions in Shared Viewing

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Robot learning has made rapid progress in control, manipulation, and navigation, yet the affective capabilities of companion robots remain largely unexplored. Watching movies together with humans provides a natural and emotionally rich setting for studying robot affective perception and response. It requires robots to understand viewer-level emotion and remain robust to first-person-view (FPV) screen observations. In RoboAffect, we introduce EgoScreen-Emotion (ESE), the first benchmark dataset for robot affective perception from shared viewing. ESE contains 224 movie trailers and 28,667 egocentric keyframes captured under 18 systematic viewing conditions (camera height, viewing distance, and horizontal angle), annotated by 5 raters with multi-label confidence scores. Inter-annotator agreement reaches Fleiss’ κ=0.34 overall and κ=0.72 on high-confidence frames. We demonstrate a significant FPV domain gap: Macro-F1 drops from 28.06 to 14.64 under Raw→FPV transfer, corresponding to a 47.8% relative decline, motivating FPV-matched training for reliable affective perception on robots. Building on ESE, we develop a multimodal long-context framework integrating 3-frame visuals, synchronized audio, and narrative summaries, trained with confidence-aware aggregation and label smoothing to handle affective ambiguity. Our full model achieves Macro-F1 32.74, reaching 84.3% of the human leave-one-out reference (38.82) and outperforming all zero-shot baselines including Gemini-2.5-Flash (23.50). We further validate the pipeline on a physical quadruped robot, demonstrating end-to-end affective perception and expression under real FPV conditions. Dataset, code, and model weights will be released for reproducibility.

Physics-Guided Biomechanical Gait Adaptation for Humanoid Locomotion on Extreme Sloped Terrains

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Nanyang Technological University

摘要原文

Model-free reinforcement learning has enabled impressive humanoid locomotion. Existing methods are dedicated to developing policies that can locomote on complex terrains, but locomotion on sloped terrain is seldom explored, requiring simultaneous stability and posture control. To address these challenges, we propose a two-stage framework with physics guidance for blind humanoid slope locomotion. In Stage I, we learn a general locomotion prior with a terrain-aligned apparent Zero Moment Point (ZMP) regularizer, evaluating balance on a local support plane rather than a world-horizontal plane. However, the trained policies on steep inclines often present inefficient crouched gaits and unnatural postures that overuse the low Center of Mass (CoM) for short-term stability. To handle these issues, we further introduce the Biomechanical Slope Gait Adapter (BSGA) in Stage II, which uses PCA-extracted macroscopic terrain descriptors as training-only critic inputs and reward-gating signals. It conditions CoM-height, joint-level gait, and swing-leg priors on the estimated slope geometry, encouraging hip-dominant uphill propulsion and knee-oriented downhill braking while keeping the deployed actor purely proprioceptive. To address the absence of simulators, we also develop a variety of uphill terrain environments for policy training based on Isaac Lab. Sim-to-real experiments on a real-world Unitree G1 show that our method mitigates crouched-gait degeneration and enables blind continuous traversal of outdoor grass slopes up to 25°, providing a physics-informed approach for robust humanoid locomotion on steep inclines. keywords: Humanoid Locomotion, Sloped Terrains, Reinforcement Learning
