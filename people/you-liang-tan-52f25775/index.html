World Action Models are Zero-shot Policies

Oral · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：NVIDIA

以联合预测未来视频与动作的 World Action Model 学习物理先验。DreamZero 研究多样化数据下的零样本任务泛化、跨构型视频迁移，以及新机器人上的少量数据适应。

摘要原文

State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models excel at semantic generalization but struggle to generalize to unseen physical motions in novel environments. We introduce DreamZero, a World Action Model (WAM) built upon a pretrained video diffusion backbone. Unlike VLAs, WAMs learn physical dynamics by predicting future world states and actions, using video as a dense representation of how the world evolves. By jointly modeling video and action, DreamZero learns diverse skills effectively from heterogeneous robot data without relying on repetitive demonstrations. This results in over 2x improvement in generalization to new tasks in new environments compared to state-of-the-art VLAs in real-robot experiments. Crucially, through model and system optimizations, we enable a 14B autoregressive video diffusion model to perform real-time closed-loop control at 7Hz.

EgoScale: Scaling Dexterous Manipulation with Diverse Egocentric Human Data

Oral · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：NVIDIA

用大规模第一人称人类操作视频预训练灵巧操作 VLA，再以轻量的人机对齐数据完成迁移。关注人类数据规模与真机表现的联系，以及低机器人数据条件下的长时任务和新手型适应。

摘要原文

Human behavior is among the most scalable sources of data for learning physical intelligence, yet how to effectively leverage it for dexterous manipulation remains unclear. While prior work demonstrates human-to-robot transfer in constrained settings, it is unclear whether large-scale human data can support fine-grained, high-degree-of-freedom dexterous manipulation. We present EgoScale, a human-to-dexterous-manipulation transfer framework built on large-scale egocentric human data. We train a Vision–Language–Action (VLA) model on over 20,854 hours of action-labeled egocentric human video—more than 20× larger than prior efforts—and uncover a log-linear scaling law between human data scale and validation loss. This validation loss strongly correlates with downstream real-robot performance, establishing large-scale human data as a predictable supervision source. Beyond scale, we introduce a simple two-stage transfer recipe: large-scale human pretraining followed by lightweight aligned human–robot mid-training. This enables strong long-horizon dexterous manipulation and one-shot task adaptation with minimal robot supervision. Our final policy improves the average success rate by 54% over a no-pretraining baseline using a 22-DoF dexterous robotic hand, and transfers effectively to robots with lower-DoF hands, indicating that large-scale human motion provides a reusable, embodiment-agnostic motor prior.

ScaleEvals: Scaling Generalist Policy Evaluation through Reset-Free Autonomous Play

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Real-world physical evaluation of generalist robot policies remains a major bottleneck: standard protocols require manual scene resets, costly human grading, and extrapolation from small fixed task suites, yielding low throughput and coarse policy diagnostics. We present ScaleEvals, a reset-free evaluation protocol that autonomously probes generalist policies through continuous play without per-rollout human intervention. ScaleEvals uses a VLM-based pipeline in which an AutoPrompter continuously generates grounded tasks and rubrics from an evolving scene, while an AutoGrader assesses task progress from multimodal observations. Each prompt is annotated with skill tags capturing action and language factors, enabling performance analysis across heterogeneous task streams. We normalize comparisons by instruction-derived difficulty and aggregate progress scores via a soft-win Bradley–Terry model with tag-stratified ANOVA, producing global policy rankings and interpretable capability diagnostics. Across five generalist policies and 1,750 real-world trajectories, ScaleEvals achieves a 3x increase in rollouts per hour per station while AutoGrader maintains 90.7% agreement with human graders. Reset-free performance rankings align with manual evaluation baselines with 0.92 (p < 0.05) Pearson correlation, while exposing capability differences that aggregate success rates obscure. These results suggest that real-world robot evaluation can scale as an autonomous, continuously running system rather than a sequence of reset-required benchmark trials.
