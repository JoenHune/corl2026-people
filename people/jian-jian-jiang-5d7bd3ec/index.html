本届研究与论文

GRACE: Group-Aware Socially Compliant Visual Navigation in Human Environments

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Visual navigation has made substantial progress in learning goal-reaching policies from egocentric observations. Most existing approaches primarily focus on geometric traversability, however, they do not explicitly account for the social constraints induced by human-populated environments where collision-free space can still be socially occupied, such as the space between walking partners or within conversational formations. To address this, we present GRACE, a group-aware framework for socially compliant visual navigation. The key insight of GRACE is to explicitly model social relations and group interaction spaces, allowing the robot to avoid paths that are physically feasible but socially undesirable. Specifically, our social perception module builds a visual pedestrian--robot graph from RGB observations and uses soft F-formation targets to supervise group-aware attention. This social representation guides a visual trajectory generator, which we further finetune using group-relative reward-weighted flow matching to preserve personal and group spaces. Real-world experiments on a Unitree Go2 demonstrate that GRACE significantly reduces group-space intrusions and collision events compared with representative baselines, while maintaining competitive navigation efficiency.
