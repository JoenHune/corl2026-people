UniFlow: A Unified Multi-Modal Policy for Robot Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Visuomotor manipulation policies face a persistent trade-off: image-only policies inherit the semantic priors of large-scale 2D pretraining but are brittle to lighting, texture, and viewpoint shift, whereas 3D-only policies are robust to such factors yet are often trained from scratch. Some recent multi-modal policies combine both streams but typically process 3D through a from-scratch encoder, preserving this asymmetry. We present UniFlow, a unified 2D+3D co-training recipe for manipulation in which 3D observations are represented as image-shaped pointmaps so that pretrained 2D backbones can be used to encode both modalities, and RGB and pointmap streams are fused by a unified diffusion transformer with per-modality modulation. On average across thirteen dexterous manipulation tasks from the challenging LBM simulation benchmark, UniFlow outperforms a strong RGB-only baseline in a distribution-shifted setting. UniFlow also outperforms the generalist VLA $\pi_{0.5}$ when averaged over five RoboTwin bimanual tasks as well as four real-world YAM tasks.

H2RBench: A Real-to-Sim Benchmark for Evaluating Human-to-Robot Transfer

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Toyota Research Institute

摘要原文

Learning robot manipulation policies from human video demonstrations constitutes a promising avenue for scalable robot learning. However, comparing different human-to-robot (H2R) transfer methods remains challenging, as existing approaches are evaluated under different settings, including differing task suites, scene layouts, object instances, and amounts of robot supervision. To address this challenge, we present H2RBench, a Real2Sim benchmark for evaluating H2R transfer methods. H2RBench provides a standardized protocol built on real human video demonstrations and simulated robot demonstrations, and includes four manipulation tasks spanning diverse interaction requirements. We evaluate multiple representative H2R transfer methods that leverage task-specific human demonstrations and limited robot data, each adopting a different strategy for bridging the embodiment gap. Using H2RBench, we systematically characterize how each method scales with the amount of human demonstrations, revealing that methods differ substantially in their ability to leverage additional human data. We further show that simulation performance is broadly predictive of real-world robot performance, with an overall Pearson correlation of $r=0.89$, Spearman correlation of $\rho=0.85$ and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) of 0.06 across method--task configurations. These results establish H2RBench as a practical and scalable benchmark for comparative H2R evaluation prior to real-world deployment.
