本届研究与论文

Physics-Guided Biomechanical Gait Adaptation for Humanoid Locomotion on Extreme Sloped Terrains

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Model-free reinforcement learning has enabled impressive humanoid locomotion. Existing methods are dedicated to developing policies that can locomote on complex terrains, but locomotion on sloped terrain is seldom explored, requiring simultaneous stability and posture control. To address these challenges, we propose a two-stage framework with physics guidance for blind humanoid slope locomotion. In Stage I, we learn a general locomotion prior with a terrain-aligned apparent Zero Moment Point (ZMP) regularizer, evaluating balance on a local support plane rather than a world-horizontal plane. However, the trained policies on steep inclines often present inefficient crouched gaits and unnatural postures that overuse the low Center of Mass (CoM) for short-term stability. To handle these issues, we further introduce the Biomechanical Slope Gait Adapter (BSGA) in Stage II, which uses PCA-extracted macroscopic terrain descriptors as training-only critic inputs and reward-gating signals. It conditions CoM-height, joint-level gait, and swing-leg priors on the estimated slope geometry, encouraging hip-dominant uphill propulsion and knee-oriented downhill braking while keeping the deployed actor purely proprioceptive. To address the absence of simulators, we also develop a variety of uphill terrain environments for policy training based on Isaac Lab. Sim-to-real experiments on a real-world Unitree G1 show that our method mitigates crouched-gait degeneration and enables blind continuous traversal of outdoor grass slopes up to 25°, providing a physics-informed approach for robust humanoid locomotion on steep inclines. keywords: Humanoid Locomotion, Sloped Terrains, Reinforcement Learning
