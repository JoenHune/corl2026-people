Not All Actions are Equal: Reliable Value Estimation for Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics

摘要原文

Offline-to-online reinforcement learning is a promising paradigm for generalizable robotic manipulation, but its full-stack complexity makes system behavior difficult to reproduce and diagnose. Within such systems, value estimation plays a central role by determining how heterogeneous data are prioritized for policy improvement. Despite its importance, a central question remains underexplored: how value-function reliability affects value-guided learning across offline pretraining and online adaptation. To answer this question, we ground our analysis in a complete offline-to-online reinforcement learning pipeline and introduce value-reliability metrics that quantify value-function quality along global task-level progress and local action-level preference, enabling us to analyze how value estimation affects downstream policy behavior. Across the extensive empirical study encompassing 240 hours of pretraining data and over 3,000 rollout trajectories, our experiments show that these metrics are closely aligned with downstream performance: More reliable value functions yield better action-quality estimates, enabling value-guided offline RL to scale more effectively than quality-agnostic behavior cloning. As value estimates become more reliable, online adaptation becomes more stable through better prioritization of high-quality rollout data. Integrating reliable value guidance through offline pretraining with online adaptation, our system achieves 86\% success on millimeter-level precise chip insertion and 84\% on generalizable block disassembly. Ultimately, we hope our findings could underscore the importance of value-guided data utilization for turning heterogeneous robotic experience into effective policy improvement. We will release the full offline-to-online RL framework and the corresponding pretraining data to promote reproducibility and further research.

RoboGene: Boosting VLA Pre-training via Diversity-Driven Agentic Framework for Real-World Task Generation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics

摘要原文

The pursuit of general-purpose robotic manipulation is hindered by the scarcity of diverse, real-world interaction data. Unlike the large-scale web vision-language data, robotic data collection is an active process incurring prohibitive physical costs. As a result, the key challenge is not merely to collect more robot data, but to obtain training data that provides informative learning knowledge by broadening behavioral coverage and capturing meaningful variations in objects, scenes, and skills. However, how to automatically curate task proposals that maximize the utility of collected robot data remains a critical yet under-explored challenge. To address this, we introduce RoboGene, an agentic framework designed to automate the generation of diverse and informative manipulation tasks across heterogeneous robotic embodiments, spanning single-arm, dual-arm, and mobile robots. RoboGene integrates three core components: (1) diversity-driven sampling for broad task coverage, (2) agentic self-reflection for task-quality assessment, and (3) human-verified feedback memory for execution-grounded validation and continual refinement. We conduct extensive quantitative analysis and large-scale real-world experiments, collecting datasets of 18k trajectories and introducing novel metrics to assess task quality in terms of feasibility and diversity. Results demonstrate that RoboGene significantly outperforms frontier foundation models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro). Furthermore, real-world experiments show that VLA models trained on data generated by RoboGene achieve higher success rates and stronger generalization, highlighting the importance of high-quality task generation. Our project is available at https://x-robogene.github.io/.

SafeDojo: Safe Reinforcement Learning for VLA via Interactive World Model

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics

摘要原文

Safe control is a prerequisite for real-world embodied intelligence, for which safe reinforcement learning has emerged as a promising paradigm. However, existing safe reinforcement learning methods either require costly real-world exploration or depend on hand-crafted safety functions. Neither scales to vision-language-action models deployed in open-world physical environments. We propose SafeDojo, the first model-based safe reinforcement learning framework for vision-language-action policies designed to learn safe actions through world model-based imagination. Specifically, SafeDojo performs online reinforcement learning on top of an interactive video world model. The world model generates action-conditioned future predictions, from which a tailored ResNet success classifier estimates per-step task progress from imagined frames and a lightweight safety head predicts per-step contact probability from latent context together with the proposed action chunk, enabling simultaneous assessment of task execution and trajectory safety. The decoupled task-reward and safety-cost signals are balanced through a Lagrangian-based constrained GRPO objective, enabling coordinated improvement of task success and safety under explicit constraints. Experiments on SafeLIBERO and real-world Franka deployment demonstrate that SafeDojo consistently outperforms inference-time safety, model-free RL, and model-based RL baselines on five key metrics, achieving over 7% average safe-success improvement across 21 tasks. Our results position world model-based safe reinforcement learning as a scalable and generalizable path toward safe embodied intelligence.

RoboAug: One Annotation to Hundreds of Scenes via Region-Contrastive Data Augmentation for Robotic Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics

摘要原文

Enhancing the generalization of robotic learning in diverse unseen environments remains a fundamental challenge. Existing approaches often rely on large-scale pretraining, which is labor-intensive and time-consuming, or semantic data augmentation methods that assume flawless upstream object detection in real-world scenarios. In this work, we propose RoboAug, a novel generative data augmentation framework that reduces reliance on large-scale pretraining and perfect visual recognition by requiring only a single image with bounding box annotations for dataset construction. Leveraging this minimal supervision, RoboAug employs pretrained generative models for precise semantic augmentation and introduces a plug-and-play region-contrastive loss to guide attention toward task-relevant regions, thereby enhancing generalization and task success rates. We conduct extensive real-world experiments on three robotic platforms—UR-5e, AgileX, and Tien Kung 2.0—covering over 35k rollouts. Experimental results show that RoboAug consistently outperforms state-of-the-art augmentation baselines. In unseen environments with varying backgrounds, distractors, and lighting conditions, RoboAug improves success rates over the no-augmentation baseline from 0.09 to 0.47 on UR-5e, 0.16 to 0.60 on AgileX, and 0.19 to 0.67 on Tien Kung 2.0. These results demonstrate the superior generalization and effectiveness of RoboAug in real-world manipulation tasks.

MeshMimic: Geometry-Aware Humanoid Motion Learning through 3D Scene Reconstruction

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Humanoid motion control has witnessed significant breakthroughs in recent years, with deep reinforcement learning (RL) emerging as a primary catalyst for achieving complex, human-like behaviors. However, the high dimensionality and intricate dynamics of humanoid robots make manual motion design impractical, leading to a heavy reliance on expensive motion capture (MoCap) data. These datasets are not only costly to acquire but also frequently lack the necessary geometric context of the surrounding physical environment. Consequently, existing motion synthesis frameworks often suffer from a decoupling of motion and scene, resulting in physical inconsistencies such as contact slippage or mesh penetration during terrain-aware tasks. In this work, we present MeshMimic, an innovative framework that bridges 3D scene reconstruction and embodied intelligence to enable humanoid robots to learn coupled "motion-terrain" interactions directly from video. By leveraging state-of-the-art 3D vision models, our framework precisely segments and reconstructs both human trajectories and the underlying 3D geometry of terrains and objects. We introduce an optimization algorithm based on kinematic consistency to extract high-quality motion data from noisy visual reconstructions, alongside a contact-invariant retargeting method that transfers human-environment interaction features to the humanoid agent. Experimental results demonstrate that MeshMimic achieves robust, highly dynamic performance across diverse and challenging terrains. Our approach proves that a low-cost pipeline utilizing only consumer-grade monocular sensors can facilitate the training of complex physical interactions, offering a scalable path toward the autonomous evolution of humanoid robots in unstructured environments.

SOLO: Stable Omni-terrain Long-Horizon Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Humans seamlessly navigate complex environments—such as winding trails, uneven stairs, and sparse stepping stones—over miles of continuous walking without losing their balance. Getting humanoid robots to achieve this long-horizon reliability requires overcoming compounding errors between perception and action. Current perceptive locomotion methods often fail during kilometer-scale deployments because they rely on lossy dense terrain representations that smooth over action-critical details, and myopic imitation objectives that lack trajectory-level credit assignment. In this paper, we present SOLO (Stable Omni-terrain Long-Horizon Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion), an end-to-end framework designed to conquer these compounding errors using only a single, noisy chest-mounted depth camera and proprioception. SOLO achieves this through two key innovations: a Query Reconstructor (QR) that explicitly retrieves precise, per-cell spatial evidence to preserve sharp terrain edges, and Trajectory-Aware MSE (TA-MSE) Distillation. By injecting next-state teacher disagreement directly into the reinforcement learning reward channel, TA-MSE unlocks explicit trajectory-level credit assignment, naturally penalizing actions that drive future state divergence. We demonstrate SOLO zero-shot on a real humanoid robot, achieving a continuous 1.5-kilometer outdoor traversal across naturally occurring stairs, slopes, and unstructured terrain. In rigorous stress tests, our system cuts terrain estimation error by up to 4× and raises traversal success on extreme sparse footholds from 3% to 96%, unlocking robust, deployable humanoid autonomy in the wild.

HAF-VLA: Hierarchical Action Flow Framework for Humanoid Vision-Language-Action Control

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics

摘要原文

Humanoid robots hold great promise as general-purpose agents in human-centered environments, yet adapting generalist vision-language-action (VLA) policies to real-world humanoid loco-manipulation remains challenging. Unlike fixed-base or wheeled manipulators, humanoids must coordinate locomotion, posture, gaze, and dual-arm manipulation over a high-dimensional whole-body action space, where inaccurate base or waist motions can easily induce unstable and aggressive upper-body compensation. We propose HAF-VLA, a hierarchical action-flow framework for humanoid VLA control. Built upon a pretrained flow-matching VLA, HAF-VLA inherits the vision-language prefix encoder and action expert weights, and adapts them to humanoid whole-body control with learnable stage embeddings and cross-stage KV-cache conditioning. Instead of generating the full action chunk in a single pass, HAF-VLA progressively denoises a shared whole-body action space: first locomotion and head commands, then waist refinement, and finally full-body manipulation. To preserve structural dependencies across stages, we introduce a cross-stage KV cache conditioning mechanism, enabling subsequent action generation stages to refine and augment their outputs based on the action representations from preceding stages. Experiment results across six real-world tasks demonstrate that our framework significantly enhances the performance of existing models in humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, demonstrating an effective pathway for adapting strong mobile-manipulation VLAs to humanoid whole-body control.
