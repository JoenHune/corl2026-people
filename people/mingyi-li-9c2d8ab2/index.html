本届研究与论文

Memory-augmented Action-conditioned World Model for Robot Manipulation

Oral · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Dalian University of Technology

摘要原文

Action-conditioned world models have emerged as a promising paradigm for robot learning, offering a scalable alternative to costly real-world experimentation by generating action-consistent video rollouts. However, a fundamental challenge remains in \emph{persistent world modeling}: due to frequent occlusions from the end-effector and rapid egocentric motion of the wrist camera, the information required to predict future observations is often insufficiently captured by the current observation. As a result, world models that rely only on the current observation may forget or hallucinate scene information visible in earlier frames, leading to inconsistent future predictions. Existing approaches mitigate this issue by enlarging the temporal context, retrieving observations with similar joint poses, or using camera field-of-view overlap as a retrieval cue. However, they often fail to retrieve informative history in dynamic manipulation scenarios. To address this limitation, we propose Ctrl-Mem, a memory-augmented multi-view action-conditioned world model. At its core, we introduce W-VMem, a 4D wrist-view-centered surfel-indexed memory that anchors historical observations to temporally evolving surface elements. By explicitly modeling when and where scene elements are observed, W-VMem enables geometry-aware retrieval of relevant history frames conditioned on future wrist camera poses. During generation, relevant history frames are selected via surfel-based rendering and scoring, providing informative and non-redundant context for prediction. Extensive experiments show that Ctrl-Mem generates more temporally consistent rollouts in complex manipulation scenarios, outperforming prior methods under a challenging replay-based evaluation protocol. Moreover, Ctrl-Mem enables reliable policy evaluation with strong correlation to real-world performance and improves policy performance on long-horizon tasks.
