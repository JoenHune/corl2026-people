本届研究与论文

EVOLVE: Egocentric Vision-based Humanoid Loco-Manipulation via Demonstration Editing

Poster · 首位署名

摘要原文

Humanoid loco-manipulation requires long-horizon coordination between locomotion and object interaction, as well as robust adaptation to diverse objects and noisy real-world observations. Recent approaches have shown promising results by learning loco-manipulation policies from reference motions or task-specific rewards, but they often struggle to generalize across objects. We propose \textbf{\textsc{Evolve}}, a framework for learning egocentric vision-based humanoid loco-manipulation through simulation-based demonstration editing and hierarchical visuomotor policy training. Given a single successful demonstration, our method adapts it to diverse objects by applying structured transformations to robot keypoint trajectories. These edited trajectories are executed by a task-agnostic low-level whole-body controller that tracks future target poses of sparse body keypoints. We then train a high-level visuomotor policy from the successful edited rollouts to enable real-world deployment. In simulation, our method generalizes across substantial object variations in size, shape, pose, and initialization, outperforming representative reference-based and reference-free baselines. We further deploy the learned policy zero-shot on real-world tasks. These results suggest that combining demonstration editing with hierarchical visuomotor control is a scalable strategy for transferring humanoid loco-manipulation skills from a single demonstration to diverse real-world objects.
