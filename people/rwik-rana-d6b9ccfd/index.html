本届研究与论文

Adapting Generalist Vehicle Models for High-Speed MPC Across Terrains

Poster · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Texas at Austin

摘要原文

High-speed off-road autonomy requires precise closed-loop control for a target vehicle while remaining robust across changing terrains. Recent forward kino-dynamic prediction foundation models suggest a promising path: start from a generalist model and specialize it to the target platform. However, effective specialization remains challenging: it often requires substantial real-world data, and models adapted to one setting can still overfit to specific terrains or driving regimes. We present OptCar (Optimum Car), a recipe for bridging the gap from generalist to specialist FKD models that preserves cross-terrain generalization and optimizes performance for a specific vehicle. OptCar fine-tunes a generalist model using limited real-world data together with targeted synthetic rollouts from environment-specific system identification. Additionally, OptCar introduces a history-conditioned dynamics adaptation module that encodes recent state-action observations into a dynamics context token. In closed-loop model predictive control (MPC) experiments across three terrains and an out-of-distribution cart-pulling task, we report the 6 m/s regime, the highest speed we evaluate and the setting in which slip dominates tracking error. On vegetation and dirt, the most slip-diverse terrain, OptCar cuts 6 m/s trajectory tracking error by roughly 55% relative to a fine-tuned AnyCar baseline, and it stays the most accurate even when an unseen cart payload changes the dynamics. With only 5 minutes of real data per terrain, OptCar is competitive with a specialist trained on 30 minutes of real road data on road, and substantially outperforms it once the terrain changes.
