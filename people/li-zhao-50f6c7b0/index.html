Learning Additively Compositional Latent Actions for Embodied AI

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Microsoft

摘要原文

Latent action models (LAMs) infer pseudo-action labels from visual transitions, providing scalable supervision from internet-scale video for embodied AI. Existing LAMs rely primarily on visual reconstruction, which pushes the latent to encode any cue predictive of the next frame, including background appearance and future shortcuts. The resulting latents entangle motion with nuisance content and their norms decouple from motion magnitude, weakening cross-environment transfer and providing poor supervision for downstream policies. We argue that these failures stem from the absence of structural priors on the latent space, and propose to endow the latent space with an explicit algebraic structure. We introduce the Additively Compositional Latent Action Model (AC-LAM), which imposes a structural constraint on the latent action space, requiring latent actions to compose additively over time. Such additivity is naturally satisfied by rigid-body motion, yet violated by many nuisance signals. We show theoretically that this single constraint induces identity, inverse, and cycle-consistency structure in the latent space, and discourages additively-separable nuisance components such as scene-constant and goal-only offsets. Empirically, we develop a diagnostic suite that probes these algebraic properties and shows that AC-LAM learns more physically meaningful, displacement-calibrated latents with markedly less scene-identity leakage than prior LAMs. The same latents translate into consistent gains on downstream policy learning across both simulated and real-robot tabletop manipulation under in- and out-of-distribution conditions.

Beyond Monotonic Progress: Retry-Supervised Value Learning for Robot Imitation

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Microsoft Research

摘要原文

Human demonstrations for robot imitation learning often contain mistakes and corrective behaviors, such as imprecise grasps, object misalignment, unstable contact, and repeated attempts. While these segments are commonly treated as noisy or suboptimal data, they provide valuable evidence about when execution deviates from a desirable path and how task feasibility can be restored. However, existing reward and value models often rely on monotonic progress assumptions, which capture coarse task advancement but may overlook local execution errors and corrective behaviors in imperfect demonstrations. In this work, we propose ReTVL (Retry-Supervised Value Learning), a framework for learning mistake-sensitive value functions from mixed-quality robot demonstrations by leveraging retry events as sparse supervision. ReTVL captures the local degradation-and-recovery structure around mistakes by combining global progress calibration with local pairwise preference learning induced by sparsely annotated retry keypoints. The learned value model is then used to reweight demonstration chunks for downstream behavior cloning, reducing the influence of harmful execution errors while preserving useful corrective behaviors. Experiments on real-robot manipulation tasks show that ReTVL produces more fine-grained value estimates than progress-based baselines and improves imitation learning from imperfect demonstrations.
