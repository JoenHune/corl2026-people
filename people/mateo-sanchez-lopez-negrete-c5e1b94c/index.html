本届研究与论文

Artificial Foveated Perception for Mitigating Shortcut Learning in Robotic Foundation Models

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：Yale University

摘要原文

Robotic foundation models have recently made substantial progress in multi-task capability, cross-embodiment transfer, and language-conditioned control. Yet deploying these models robustly across diverse real-world settings remains difficult, in part because policies often fail to distinguish between causally relevant visual structure and spurious scene-level correlations. For instance, a policy trained to pick up a mug may fail when the table is shifted, the room becomes dimmer, or previously unseen distractors appear nearby. We identify this failure mode as \textit{shortcut learning}: the tendency of a model to exploit predictive but non-causal correlations in the training distribution rather than the task-relevant visual evidence that determines successful action. Although shortcut learning has been extensively studied in computer vision and broader machine learning, its role in robotic foundation models remains comparatively underexplored. In this paper, we study shortcut learning as a central obstacle to scalable robotic foundation models and propose Artificial Foveated Perception (AFP) as a direct intervention in the perception pipeline. AFP is a lightweight, policy-agnostic module that takes the same vision and language inputs as existing Vision-Language-Action and World Action Model pipelines and predicts task-conditioned masks over relevant objects, the robot, and other action-critical regions. These masks can be used for visual preprocessing, token filtering, attention modulation, or auxiliary grounding losses, enabling AFP to suppress task-irrelevant visual evidence without modifying the core policy architecture. We evaluate AFP across state-of-the-art robotic foundation models and show that foveating perception reduces fine-tuning time, suppresses overfitting, and improves generalization under environmental perturbations. Through ablations over integration modes and mask usage, we further show that these gains arise from directing policy learning and inference toward task-relevant visual regions. These results suggest that task-conditioned foveated perception is a practical mechanism for making robotic foundation models more robust, data-efficient, and scalable.
