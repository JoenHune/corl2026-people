本届研究与论文

From Foundation VLM to Versatile Driving VLM: A Triplet Incremental Training Approach

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Adapting general Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to Autonomous Driving (AD) is thought to require heavy 3D spatial priors or intrusive architectural overhauls to compensate for a lack of spatial grounding. In this work, we present a counterintuitive pilot study: off-the-shelf general VLMs already exhibit remarkable adaptability across diverse driving tasks, while precise metric depth remains the primary missing link, a bottleneck readily addressable via onboard sensors and depth foundation models. This insight reveals that instead of complex over-engineering, general VLMs merely require a targeted geometric catalyst to bridge the spatial gap. To this end, we introduce the Triplet Incremental Training Framework, a non-intrusive paradigm that seamlessly instills 3D geometric awareness into a general VLM backbone while fully preserving its open-world capabilities. Driven by automatically constructed (image, depth, QA pair) triplets, our framework operates via a unified tri-stream training pipeline: an unfrozen primary VLM stream to adapt to driving tasks, an auxiliary depth stream that guides the visual backbone to implicitly imagine 3D scene geometry via explicit geometric constraints, and a frozen twin VLM stream employing logit-level KL distillation to prevent semantic drift. Crucially, the auxiliary depth branch is discarded during deployment, ensuring the enhanced geometric awareness comes with zero inference overhead. We evaluate our framework across four major benchmarks targeting four diverse capabilities spanning 3D spatial perception, driving-oriented language reasoning, end-to-end planning, and general multimodal understanding. Our approach outperforms state-of-the-art driving VLMs, rivals domain-specific expert models in 3D perception, and successfully retains its original general reasoning capabilities. This demonstrates that Triplet Incremental Training offers a unified, deployment-ready paradigm for transforming general foundation models into robust driving intelligences.
