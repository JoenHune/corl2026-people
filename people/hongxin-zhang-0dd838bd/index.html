本届研究与论文

HARP-Bench: Benchmarking Robot Manipulation Around Active Humans

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Recent robot manipulation benchmarks have enabled systematic evaluation of general-purpose robot policies across diverse objects, scenes, instructions, and embodiments. However, most existing benchmarks still assume that the robot is the only active agent and that task semantics are determined primarily by the instruction and object state. This assumption overlooks a common source of real-world variation: humans actively sharing the workspace. In such settings, humans may reveal an implicit goal, temporarily change object availability, complete part of the task, or constrain safe robot motion while working nearby. We introduce HARP-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating robot manipulation policies in workspaces shared with active humans. HARP-Bench treats human activity as part of the manipulation problem, rather than as background scene variation. It organizes human-present manipulation into four conditions: Cueing, Interruption, Contribution, and Co-presence. Each condition is instantiated as paired variants of everyday manipulation tasks, enabling controlled comparison between standard manipulation and human-present manipulation. HARP-Bench also includes an interactive data collection pipeline for gathering demonstrations in dynamic human-present settings. We evaluate a diverse set of policies on HARP-Bench, including VLA models, imitation-learning methods, and heuristic policies. Our results show that human-present task variations pose a significant challenge for current robot policies. We further show that training on demonstrations collected with HARP-Bench improves performance under these human-present conditions. Real-world experiments further confirm the validity of HARP-Bench, and our generated data can improve policy behavior when humans actively affect task execution.
