RoboDreamer: Anticipatory Humanoid Locomotion with Predictive State-Space Models

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Humanoid locomotion requires control policies that remain stable under imperfect sensing while capturing long-term temporal dependencies for consistent and adaptive movement. Conventional end-to-end policies based on multi-layer perceptrons lack explicit temporal memory and often struggle to recover from sensor dropouts, whereas Transformer-based policies introduce substantial computational overhead that limits their use in high-frequency real-time control. To address these challenges, we propose RoboDreamer, a framework built upon Mamba's selective state-space mechanism for efficient long-horizon temporal modeling. Through randomized temporal masking during training, RoboDreamer learns to recover missing observations and predict future states, which supports robust control under partial sensory corruption. Leveraging this capability, RoboDreamer further enables action chunking, generating multi-step actions within a single inference pass to improve control smoothness. Experiments show that RoboDreamer outperforms MLP and Transformer baselines in robustness and efficiency, maintaining stable locomotion under masked observations while supporting real-time deployment for high-frequency humanoid control.
