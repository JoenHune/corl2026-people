本届研究与论文

What is the Better Curriculum: Controller-Shaped Grasping Behavior for Contact Force-Sensitive Manipulation

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

How should a robot learn to manipulate objects so fragile that sub-Newton contact forces can cause irreversible damage? Prior visuo-tactile policy learning typically treats tactile sensing as an additional modality to integrate into the policy, for example by fusing tactile inputs with vision or using execution-time adaptation. In direct-contact force-sensitive manipulation, however, the main bottleneck arises earlier: during teleoperated data collection, contact force is not directly observable, and visual feedback is too delayed to reliably maintain the narrow force range required for stable grasping. We address this problem with a tactile reflex controller used during collection, producing teleoperated demonstrations with controller-shaped grasping behavior. These shaped demonstrations form a better curriculum for tactile-free policy learning. The effect is clearest on Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), where the learned policy matches the teacher's closing profile and achieves 95% stable grasps on the nominal plastic-cup task. The same data intervention also improves in-distribution stability and transfer to an unseen paper-cup variant on π₀.₅. Under randomized external disturbance, however, the same reflex-data π₀.₅ policy fails in 45% of policy-only trials, whereas deterministic reflex arbitration preserves the grasp with no failures. These results reveal a new role for tactile feedback in force-sensitive manipulation: rather than integrating tactile into the policy, we use it as a collection-time teacher that shapes grasping behavior in demonstrations for policy learning, while disturbance rejection remains controller-dependent, revealing the boundary of tactile-free policy.
