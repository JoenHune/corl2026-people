Closing the Representational Gap for VLAs in Dynamic Settings

Oral · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of California, Irvine

摘要原文

VLA models are conditioned on a single observation at inference time, a design that suffices for quasi-static manipulation but creates two representational failures in dynamic settings. The first is motion ambiguity: a snapshot encodes configuration, not how the scene is evolving, so the policy cannot anticipate where a moving target will be. The second is state aliasing: perceptually similar observations at different task phases can demand opposite actions, producing jitter and mistimed commitments even on tasks the policy nominally understands. We show that these two failures are separable and that each has a natural fix: a motion summary from a frozen video foundation model separates scene dynamics from appearance without task-specific supervision, while a compact proprioceptive history disambiguates sequential task phases without per-task engineering. TEMPO adds these as minimal inputs to a pretrained VLA without architectural modification. On four dynamic bimanual tasks, TEMPO improves Bottle Handover success from 44% to 74% and is the only system to succeed on tasks where state-of-the-art asynchronous baselines score 0% due to unresolved state aliasing. Probing and ablation analyses confirm that each signal is individually necessary, suggesting that what limits VLAs in dynamic settings is not model capacity but the right temporal context.

GLIDE: Guardrails for Learning from Infeasible Demonstrations Efficiently

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of California, Irvine

摘要原文

Behavior cloning for robot manipulation is based on expert demonstration datasets. However, for tasks that require dynamic stability, precise contact timing, or dexterous coordination, human teleoperators may find it hard or even impossible to collect data. We study this infeasible-demonstration regime and propose GLIDE, a framework that infers task-specific failure points and converts them into executable guardrails for data collection and policy deployment. Given a task description, GLIDE writes guardrails that use robot state to filter teleoperation and policy commands, constrain failure-prone actions, and iteratively improve from closed-loop dataset feedback. Across Tomato plate transfer, Marker handover & stand, and Wine serving tasks, GLIDE improves data collection efficiency over raw VR teleoperation and manually designed guardrails. After refinement, GLIDE raises data-collection efficiency from 0-10% to 70-90% across the three tasks. During policy execution, guarded deployment raises Tomato plate transfer policy success from 0% to 60-70%, enabling efficient learning on mixed-quality demonstrations. These results show that GLIDE guardrails can support policy learning when direct demonstrations are unreliable.
