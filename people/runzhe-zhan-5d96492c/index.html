本届研究与论文

FACA: Force-Aware Contact Adaptation for Task-Parameterized Robotic Assembly

Poster · 共同署名

摘要原文

Enabling robots to adapt across related contact-rich assembly tasks remains challenging. Policies trained in simulation with diverse geometries and task parameters often degrade under real execution due to contact-model mismatch, residual alignment error, friction variation, and force-response differences. We introduce FACA, a task-residual factorized contact world model for parameterized axial assembly. FACA separates known nominal task descriptors from hidden execution-time contact residuals. The task descriptor defines the nominal assembly prior, including clearance, part scale, insertion depth, and task type, while recent force/contact history, proprioception, and action history update a latent contact belief over residual contact state. This belief conditions task-dependent latent dynamics, value estimation, and action selection, and future rollout proceeds from the belief state without assuming access to future measured force. We evaluate FACA on a compact but controlled axial assembly family rather than claiming full AutoMate benchmark coverage. The experiments are organized along two axes: generalization across known task parameters and adaptation to hidden residual/contact variations that are not provided as privileged inputs. When target-domain data are available, sparse autonomous rollouts or human correction snippets are used only as transition data to calibrate a lightweight residual dynamics adapter while keeping the base task-conditioned model and policy fixed. The experiments test whether FACA can better handle continuous task-parameter changes and difficult-to-encode contact residuals under the same low-level control interface.
