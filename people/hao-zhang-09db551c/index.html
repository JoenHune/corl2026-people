Collaborative Planning for UAV-UGV Teams under Operational Constraints via Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion

Spotlight · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Massachusetts Amherst

摘要原文

Collaborative planning under operational constraints is essential for heterogeneous robot teams in large-scale missions. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) provide rapid area coverage but are limited by onboard energy, while Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) can serve as mobile recharging stations yet are constrained by road-network traversability. Effective collaboration must enforce operational constraints during execution while enabling parallel, coordinated actions. Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) have shown promise through token unmasking but have two key limitations: entropy-guided scheduling assumes distributional alignment and reliance on differentiable constraints for diffusion-time optimization. We propose Masked Error-bounded Neuro-Symbolic Diffusion (MEND), which formulates collaborative planning as a conditional masked generative process to generate context-aware action sequences for UAV-UGV teams. MEND introduces a neuro-symbolic diffusion paradigm that constrains conditional masked diffusion policies at execution by reweighting the learned action distribution with a logic-induced admissibility indicator, thereby enforcing compliance with operational constraints. MEND introduces an error-bounded inference scheduling at execution that constructs the unmasking schedule by selecting positions with minimal predictive entropy over the constraint-aware action distribution, enhancing collaborative planning optimality under operational constraints. Extensive experimental results in simulation, on real-world maps, and with physical robot teams demonstrate that MEND advances UAV-UGV collaborative planning under operational constraints.

ExtremControl: Low-Latency Humanoid Teleoperation with Scaled Velocity Feedforward

Poster · 共同署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of Massachusetts Amherst

摘要原文

Building a low-latency humanoid teleoperation system is essential for collecting diverse reactive and dynamic demonstrations. However, end-to-end latencies in existing approaches consistently hover around 200ms, roughly equal to human reaction time, that severely limits responsiveness and prevents tasks requiring rapid feedback. We identify position-only PD control as the root cause of this latency floor and propose ExtremControl, a low-latency whole-body control framework that: (1) incorporates scaled velocity feedforward in low-level control, reducing tracking delay by approximately 70ms with a provably optimal feedforward ratio; (2) operates directly on SE(3) poses of selected rigid links, primarily humanoid extremities, to avoid joint-space retargeting in the control loop. To minimize retargeting time, we derive a closed-form Cartesian-space mapping that uses a single anthropomorphic calibration step to translate human motion into humanoid link targets, accommodating operators with varying body proportions. Furthermore, we provide a theoretical framework for selecting control hyperparameters, and validate the effectiveness of velocity feedforward control in the reinforcement learning for locomotion setting through experiments in both simulation and the real world. Building on ExtremControl, we implement a teleoperation system supporting both optical motion capture and VR-based tracking, achieving end-to-end latency as low as 50ms --- well beneath the threshold at which system delay adds a full human reaction cycle to the control loop. This enables a qualitatively new class of reactive behaviors, including ping-pong ball balancing, juggling, and real-time return, that are beyond the capability of existing teleoperation systems.
