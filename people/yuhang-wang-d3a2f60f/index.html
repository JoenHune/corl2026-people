本届研究与论文

Observe Before You Alert: Belief-State Driver Alerting with Vision–Language Models

Poster · 首位署名

该篇个人署名单位：University of South Florida

摘要原文

Driver alerting from dashcam video is a sequential decision problem under partial observability: a system must warn early enough to support driver response, while avoiding frequent nuisance alerts. Existing approaches often reduce this problem to binary risk prediction, forcing a thresholded silent-or-alert decision even when visual evidence is incomplete. We propose VLAlert, a vision-language framework that formulates driver alerting as a tri-action policy over Silent, Observe, and Alert. The Observe action serves as an internal evidence-gathering state, allowing the system to defer uncertain warnings and adapt its subsequent observation window before issuing a driver-facing alert. VLAlert adapts a 4B vision-language model to generate structured safety-evidence spans and uses hidden states pooled from these spans as compact belief representations for danger estimation and policy prediction. To evaluate alerting beyond threshold-free anticipation metrics, we construct VLAlert-Bench, a unified per-tick benchmark from multiple driving-alert datasets, and introduce DAUS, a driver-aware utility score that jointly measures ranking, video coverage, false-alert burden, and lead-time utility. Experiments show that VLAlert achieves the best overall operating-point utility among representative video and VLM baselines. Ablations further indicate that structured belief-span pooling improves alert separability and that retaining Observe as a separate action provides useful supervision for uncertain pre-alert states.
